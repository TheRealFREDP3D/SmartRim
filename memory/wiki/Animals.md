**Animals** are a type of nonplayable, nonhuman pawn in Rimworld with their own [needs](/wiki/Needs "Needs"), [stats](/wiki/Stats "Stats"), [capacities](/wiki/Capacities "Capacities"), and actions. They come in dozens of species, all of which can be wild or [tamed](/wiki/Taming "Taming"). Wild animals occasionally spawn at the map edges according to the [biome](/wiki/Biomes "Biomes") and sometimes from random events. 

Animals usually wander the map aimlessly and feed when hungry, even eating player-grown [plants](/wiki/Plants "Plants"). Like humans, animals don't require water and generate [filth](/wiki/Filth "Filth"). All animals will fight back if melee combat is initiated. With just a little luck, even a small [rat](/wiki/Rat "Rat") or [squirrel](/wiki/Squirrel "Squirrel") can [take down](/wiki/Downed "Downed") and even kill a casually armored colonist. The most dangerous species regularly hunt humans or can immediately turn [manhunter](/wiki/Manhunter "Manhunter") out of revenge. Some animals can be [trained](/wiki/Animal_husbandry#Training "Animal husbandry") to guard your colonists and attack enemies. [Friendly fire](/wiki/Combat#Friendly_fire "Combat") can happen with domesticated animals.

Animals are an important source of [food](/wiki/Food "Food") by the meat they provide once [hunted](/wiki/Orders#Hunt "Orders") or slaughtered, and subsequently [butchered](/wiki/Butcher_table "Butcher table"). 

Colonists assigned to [handle](/wiki/Work#Handle "Work") receive 90 XP towards their [animals skill](/wiki/Skills#Animals "Skills") per training or taming attempt. When tamed "[cute](/wiki/Cute "Cute")" animals nuzzle a colonist, the non-[psychopath](/wiki/Psychopath "Psychopath") colonist receives a +4 _Nuzzled_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 1 [day](/wiki/Time "Time"). Animals can also [form bonds](/wiki/Animal_husbandry#Bonding "Animal husbandry") with colonists, providing a permanent +5 _[Bonded animal]'s master_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") as long as said colonist is assigned as a bonded animal's master, or a −3 _Not [Bonded animal]'s master_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") otherwise. These moods don't apply to [psychopath](/wiki/Psychopath "Psychopath") colonists. 

Sometimes, [humans](/wiki/Human "Human") act like animals in a specific encounter [event](/wiki/Events#Wild_.28wo.29man_wanders_in "Events") or unfortunate [mental break](/wiki/Mental_break#Run_wild "Mental break"), but revert to normal colonist behavior if successfully (re)tamed. 

## Animals tab[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Animals tab")]

The Animals tab is in the menu bar at the bottom of the screen. The default hotkey is [`F4`](/wiki/Controls "Controls"). The Animals tab lists all the colony animals. From left to right, displayed information is as follows: 

  * Double-clicking an animal's name will center the map to that animal.
  * Gender, Age, and Life Stage are displayed
  * Pregnant or Sterilized
  * A button lists the animal's master, if it has one. Click the button to assign a new master. Bonded status is also displayed.
  * If the animal is designated to follow its master when drafted or doing field work.
  * A "Slaughter" checkbox column allows easy slaughtering of multiple tamed animals at once.
  * Checkbox columns for each trainable skill: **Tame, Guard, Rescue, Attack,** and **Haul** for the management of animal training.
  * [Allowed areas](/wiki/Zone/Area#Allowed_area "Zone/Area") are listed here. Animals stay in their assigned areas unless designated to follow their master.



Most of the above information is also displayed in the animal's inspect pane. 

### Allowed areas[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Allowed areas")]

One of the best ways to control animals is to assign them an [allowed area](/wiki/Allowed_area "Allowed area"). Allowed areas are created via the Expand allowed area function in the Architect>Zone menu. Zoning prevents animals from wandering into danger and eating things they shouldn't be. Be certain to designate them an [animal sleeping spot](/wiki/Animal_sleeping_spot "Animal sleeping spot") and ensure they have access to a [food](/wiki/Food "Food") source. Some species (most ungulates) cannot be assigned an area and must instead be roped by a colonist into a [pen](/wiki/Pen "Pen"). 

## Appearance[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Appearance")]

Most animal sprites do not show limbs, just like human sprites. Some animals have different appearances between males and females. Some animals have a different sprite for infant and adult life stages, like the [chicken](/wiki/Chicken "Chicken"). 

## Behavior[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Behavior")]

  * **Wandering:** Animals will wander the accessible map, their [pen](/wiki/Pen "Pen"), or their assigned [zone](/wiki/Zone "Zone") when their needs are satisfied, e.g. hunger and sleep. Some wild animals tend to stay together in a herd. Tamed animals may cross [doors](/wiki/Door "Door"), [fences](/wiki/Fence "Fence") and [barricades](/wiki/Barricade "Barricade") in their assigned area, unlike [pen](/wiki/Pen "Pen") animals. Note that pen animals that have had a [sentience catalyst](/wiki/Sentience_catalyst "Sentience catalyst")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC") can cross fences and barricades. Wild animals cannot cross doors but can cross fences and barricades if their tamed, baseline counterparts can.
  * **Filth:** While walking on constructed floors, some animals produce animal [filth](/wiki/Filth "Filth"). The Filth rate stat is proportional to the body size and wildness stats. Farm animals tend to produce massive amounts of filth. This can be mitigated by [straw matting](/wiki/Straw_matting "Straw matting") floors or by simply keeping animals outdoors on natural terrain.
  * **Roaming:** Tame pen animals will occasionally attempt to leave the map if not in a pen or if a pen door is left open. Their escape frequency is determined by the [stat](/wiki/Stat "Stat") roam interval. Roaming is disabled for animals that have had a [sentience catalyst](/wiki/Sentience_catalyst "Sentience catalyst")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC") used on them.
  * **Moving** : the animal is moving into its assigned area.
  * Consuming food: animals attempt to eat a nearby [food](/wiki/Food "Food") source when hungry. Herbivores move to find mature plants and carnivores [hunt](/wiki/Animals#predation "Animals"). Animals will keep searching for food until their hunger bar reaches satiation.
  * [Mating](/wiki/Animals#Mating "Animals"): male and female adults of the same species occasionally mate, which may result in a female pregnancy.
  * **[Sleeping](/wiki/Sleep "Sleep")** : when tired or wounded, tamed animals prefer to sleep in an [animal bed](/wiki/Animal_bed "Animal bed") or animal sleeping spot.
  * Attacking: All animals can only melee attack. They will defend themselves in melee combat, and some animals can turn [manhunter](/wiki/Manhunter "Manhunter") and chase your colonists. A text warning pops up on the screen when a colonist is ordered to interact with a potentially dangerous wild animal.
  * Fleeing: all animals have a chance of erratically fleeing from ranged attacks hitting a target in their immediate vicinity. Animals do not flee predators or melee attacks. Animals do not leave their pen or assigned area when fleeing.
  * Hauling: some animals, like the [husky](/wiki/Husky "Husky"), can be [trained](/wiki/Animals#training "Animals") to haul items, similar to how colonists haul items.
  * Following master: some animals, like the [elephant](/wiki/Elephant "Elephant"), can be trained to follow their master when drafted or doing field work (mining or hunting).
  * Nuzzling: Certain tame animals will occasionally nuzzle your colonists. Animals can even nuzzle patients in bed. A colonist who is nuzzled receives a 4 _Nuzzled_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 1 [day](/wiki/Time "Time"). The following animals can nuzzle: [cat](/wiki/Cat "Cat"), [guinea pig](/wiki/Guinea_pig "Guinea pig"), [husky](/wiki/Husky "Husky"), [labrador retriever](/wiki/Labrador_retriever "Labrador retriever"), [monkey](/wiki/Monkey "Monkey"), and [yorkshire terrier](/wiki/Yorkshire_terrier "Yorkshire terrier").



### Mating[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Mating")]

Female animals do not ever attempt to initiate mating themselves. Males will only mate with females in the same assigned area. Wild animals do not mate. Incest is common and has no effect on animal health. 

There is either a 1/12 or 1/8 (depending on species) chance per hour that an awake, non-sterilized male will search for a non-pregnant/non-fertilized, non-sterilized, awake female of the same species within 30 tiles to initiate mating. The female then has a 50% chance to become [pregnant](/wiki/Pregnant "Pregnant") in the case of gestational animals or a 100% chance to become fertilized in the case of [egg](/wiki/Egg "Egg")-laying animals. 

In order to maximize the rate of offspring for a given population size of adults, the ideal is that the moment one female becomes pregnant or fertilized, another gives birth or lays an egg and becomes available to be mated. If there was no randomness involved in mating, the female:male ratio that would achieve this is given by `awake_proportion × gestation_time / (2 × mate_mtb)` for gestational animals, or `awake_proportion × egg_interval / mate_mtb` for egg-laying animals. As the proportion of [time spent awake](/wiki/Rest "Rest") can be approximated to be about `2/3` for a rest effectiveness of 0.8, and `mate_mtb` is usually 12 hours, this can be simplified to `2/3 × gestation_time` or `4/3 × egg_interval` in most cases. 

However, as the randomness involved with mating becomes more significant with smaller population sizes, slightly more males will be desired for smaller populations. On the contrary, animals that can be milked will want more females than this ratio suggests, as the gains from milk will offset the losses from time spent not pregnant. Animals that can lay unfertilized eggs like the [chicken](/wiki/Chicken "Chicken") can similarly afford a higher female ratio. 

### Aggression[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Aggression")]

Most animals in Rimworld are dangerous to [hunt](/wiki/Hunt "Hunt") or [tame](/wiki/Tame "Tame"). A warning message will pop-up when a colonist is ordered to interact with a dangerous wild animal. 

The Revenge chance on harm stat is the chance an animal will turn [manhunter](/wiki/Manhunter "Manhunter") when harmed by a member of your colony. It is three times higher for close-ranged attacks, like that of a [machine pistol](/wiki/Machine_pistol "Machine pistol"). For an animal like an [ostrich](/wiki/Ostrich "Ostrich") with a 100% revenge chance, it will always turn manhunter after being hurt. Most animals also have a [revenge chance on tame fail](/wiki/Animals#revenge_chance_on_tame_fail "Animals") stat which dictates how likely the animal is to attack after a failed taming attempt. 

Sometimes, the entire herd of wild animals will take revenge on your colonists. They may all get turned [manhunter](/wiki/Manhunter "Manhunter") by a psychic wave [event](/wiki/Event "Event") and attack any humans they can reach. Manhunters may even pick fights with your tamed animals.

### Predation[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Predation")]

[![Predator wildlife column icon.png](/images/3/34/Predator_wildlife_column_icon.png)](/wiki/File:Predator_wildlife_column_icon.png)

Most carnivores are predators who will hunt smaller species (except domestic dogs who never hunt). When hungry, a predator will prefer an easy meal. They'll first attempt to eat meals, meat, and other food types within their diet. Otherwise they will feed upon downed animals or fresh corpses. 

When a hungry predator has no other food nearby, they will hunt, kill and consume almost any animal smaller than them, including your tamed animals and your colonists (and certainly [children](/wiki/Children "Children")[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC")). This can especially be a problem on maps with little wildlife, like on [Ice sheets](/wiki/Biomes#Ice_sheet "Biomes") when a [polar bear](/wiki/Polar_bear "Polar bear") wanders in. Predators always avoid [boomrats](/wiki/Boomrat "Boomrat") and [boomalopes](/wiki/Boomalope "Boomalope"). 

Predator attacks usually blind or stun their prey, leaving the victim unable to fight back. Unlike most other enemies, predators continue attacking after their prey is downed to finish off their target. If they do down your colonist or livestock, immediately order the nearest comrade to rescue the downed pawn. It's the difference between life and [death](/wiki/Death "Death"). 

Predators include the [Arctic fox](/wiki/Arctic_fox "Arctic fox"), [Arctic wolf](/wiki/Arctic_wolf "Arctic wolf"), [Cat](/wiki/Cat "Cat"), [Cobra](/wiki/Cobra "Cobra"), [Cougar](/wiki/Cougar "Cougar"), [Fennec fox](/wiki/Fennec_fox "Fennec fox"), [Grizzly bear](/wiki/Grizzly_bear "Grizzly bear"), [Lynx](/wiki/Lynx "Lynx"), [Panther](/wiki/Panther "Panther"), [Polar bear](/wiki/Polar_bear "Polar bear"), [Red fox](/wiki/Red_fox "Red fox"), [Timber wolf](/wiki/Timber_wolf "Timber wolf"), [Warg](/wiki/Warg "Warg"), [Alligator](/wiki/Alligator "Alligator")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"), [Bog hound](/wiki/Bog_hound "Bog hound")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"), [Greatwolf](/wiki/Greatwolf "Greatwolf")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"), [Scimitar cat](/wiki/Scimitar_cat "Scimitar cat")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"), [Tiger](/wiki/Tiger "Tiger")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"), [Wolverine](/wiki/Wolverine "Wolverine")[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC"). 

If you successfully hide all colonists and tamed animals away from a predator's reach, it will hunt any other available wildlife. 

It is not possible to view the Needs tab of a wild animal, so you may have to deduce whether or not a predator is hungry based on its behavior. They sometimes remain near the area where they last killed and ate an animal for about a day. Bloodstains or partially-consumed animal corpses on the ground are a fairly reliable guide, as well as a source of free leather and leftover food. Be careful not to let a hauler take away a predator's food before it has finished eating. It will still be hungry and will hunt your colonist instead. 

Remember that [fences](/wiki/Fence "Fence") and [fence gates](/wiki/Fence_gate "Fence gate") do not count as impassable for all animals, some predators can climb fences. 

### Body heat[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Body heat")]

Main article: [Body heat](/wiki/Body_heat "Body heat")

Animals will give out body heat, slightly heating up their surroundings. This is insignificant most of the time, except in enclosed, densely-packed barns. The heat can become a problem in warm weather or hot biomes but a benefit in cold biomes or during winter. For detailed information, see [Body heat](/wiki/Body_heat "Body heat"). 

## Reproduction[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Reproduction")]

Animals are born by live birth or by hatching from a fertilized egg. 

### Live birth[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Live birth")]

    

See also: [Pregnancy](/wiki/Pregnancy "Pregnancy")

Female Animals which give live birth (e.g. mammals) have a chance of becoming [pregnant](/wiki/Pregnant "Pregnant") after mating with a male. The **gestation time** stat specifies how many days a species' pregnancy will last. A pregnant animal suffering from [malnutrition](/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition") or injuries may miscarry, but spontaneous abortions are not possible in game. Miscarriages are noted by an in-game message and loss of the baby, but the mother is otherwise unharmed and may reproduce normally again. Pregnancies may also be aborted through [surgery](/wiki/Surgery "Surgery"). 

For the first 600 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (10 secs) the pregnancy condition will be invisible, after which point a message will come up mentioning the pregnancy and the following hediffs become visible. 

Stage | Begins at | Symptoms   
---|---|---  
**Early-stage** | >0 Severity | 

  * Vomiting (_MTB of 2.5 days_)

  
**Middle-stage** | >0.333 Severity | 

  * -15% [Moving](/wiki/Moving "Moving")

  
**Late-stage** | >0.666 Severity | 

  * Vomiting (_MTB of 5 days_)
  * -30% [Moving](/wiki/Moving "Moving")

  
_**Birth**_ | 1.0 Severity | 

  * Symptoms end
  * Offspring is born

  
  
Live births occur at the end of the gestation time. They produce [filth](/wiki/Filth "Filth") in the form of amniotic fluid, but no blood loss or damage occurs to the mother or baby. A species' **litter size** is a probability curve of the number of offspring possible from one pregnancy 

### Eggs[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Eggs")]

Egg-laying animals include the [Alligator](/wiki/Alligator "Alligator"), [Bluebird](/wiki/Bluebird "Bluebird"), [Cassowary](/wiki/Cassowary "Cassowary"), [Chicken](/wiki/Chicken "Chicken"), [Cobra](/wiki/Cobra "Cobra"), [Crow](/wiki/Crow "Crow"), [Duck](/wiki/Duck "Duck"), [Emu](/wiki/Emu "Emu"), [Flamingo](/wiki/Flamingo "Flamingo"), [Goose](/wiki/Goose "Goose"), [Heron](/wiki/Heron "Heron"), [Iguana](/wiki/Iguana "Iguana"), [Macaw](/wiki/Macaw "Macaw"), [Monitor lizard](/wiki/Monitor_lizard "Monitor lizard"), [Ostrich](/wiki/Ostrich "Ostrich"), [Peafowl](/wiki/Peafowl "Peafowl"), [Penguin](/wiki/Penguin "Penguin"), [Quail](/wiki/Quail "Quail"), [Sea turtle](/wiki/Sea_turtle "Sea turtle"), [Sparrow](/wiki/Sparrow "Sparrow"), [Swan](/wiki/Swan "Swan"), [Tortoise](/wiki/Tortoise "Tortoise"), [Turkey](/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Vulture](/wiki/Vulture "Vulture") as of 1.1. Females become fertilized after mating with males, causing them to lay fertilized eggs. Most animals will not lay unfertilized eggs, with the exception of [chickens](/wiki/Chicken "Chicken"), [ducks](/wiki/Duck "Duck"), and [geese](/wiki/Goose "Goose"). 

Fertilized eggs display their progress on the inspect pane and hatch when ready. 

Fertilized eggs can be ruined by temperature if not kept within their safe temperature range of 0 °C – 50 °C (32 °F – 122 °F). 

    Eggs start to freeze below 0 °C (32 °F).
    Eggs start to overheat above 50 °C (122 °F).

The inspect pane will indicate 'Overheating' or 'Freezing' followed by a percentage rising up to 100%. It increases by 0.003% per 1 °C (1.8 °F) outside the safe temperature range each [tick](/wiki/Tick "Tick"). This status is halted when safe temperate is restored, but it is not reset - if the temperature is unsafe again, it will pick up where it left off. If it reaches 100% the egg is 'ruined by temperature' and will not hatch even if returned to a suitable area. A ruined egg still has full nutritional value and can be used to make a meal (or eaten raw, with a mood debuff). 

### Sterilization[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Sterilization")]

    

Main article: [Sterilization](/wiki/Sterilization "Sterilization")

Animals can be sterilized by way of an operation to prevent them even attempting to breed with another animal. As such they will never reproduce, although existing pregnancies will not be terminated by the procedure.

## Health[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Health")]

Animals have the same set-up as humans when it comes to health, minus the ability to operate on them (except euthanasia)(The option to amputate an infected limb can become available once infection sets in, at least on Thrumbos—assuming other animals as well). They feel pain, and have all of the different health stats that human pawns possess. Animals can become addicted to beer, and they suffer the same negative health alcohol effects as humans. Keep them away from alcohol. Animals need to have an animal bed (or sleeping spot) in order to be healed. 

Like humans, they have a life expectancy, and are affected by [chronic diseases](/wiki/Ailments "Ailments"). There is no way to cure them in the base game other than [Healer mech serum](/wiki/Healer_mech_serum "Healer mech serum"), which would be incredibly expensive. 

A tamed animal that requires tending will find the nearest animal bed or animal sleeping spot in their [allowed area](/wiki/Allowed_area "Allowed area") and rest there until it is either fully healed or dead. Pawns assigned to doctoring will tend its wounds or illnesses and feed it, just as they would do for a human pawn. If all human colonists are absent or unable to care for a sick animal, it can die of starvation even if there is food nearby and it is capable of walking. The animal can be forced to stand up by removing the medical designation from any medical animal beds in the animal's allowed area or pen. 

Animals have a [Toxic Resistance](/wiki/Toxic_Resistance "Toxic Resistance") of 50% by default, compared to the [human](/wiki/Human "Human") default of 0%. 

## Hunting[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Hunting")]

    

See also: [Hunt](/wiki/Hunt "Hunt")

[![Hunt.png](/images/thumb/e/e1/Hunt.png/42px-Hunt.png)](/wiki/File:Hunt.png) Wild animals may be marked for hunting, done by [hunters](/wiki/Work#Hunt "Work") with ranged weapons, who will proceed to shoot them at maximum range, before executing them with a neck cut when they are downed (except explosive animals). After killing their target, they will haul the carcass to a [stockpile zone](/wiki/Stockpile_zone "Stockpile zone") even if they are naturally incapable of hauling, but will not start hauling it again if they're interrupted. 

Animals harmed by hunting that were not killed yet may become enraged and if its a pack type, its full horde may turn hostile against the entire colony. If they are non-aggressive animals they usually flee instead. 

Hunting may take longer during bad weather since there's a shooting modifier while it's raining or snowing that makes it more likely for shots to miss. 

  * Fog (with or without rain): hit chance multiplier is 50%.
  * Rain or snow: hit chance multiplier is 80%.



To mark animals to be hunted use one of the following methods: 

  * Click Wildlife, Click the first red X to the right of the animal you want to hunt and change it to a green check mark
  * Click Orders, Hunt, then click one or more individual animals.
  * Click Orders, Hunt, then click and drag a box to surround and select multiple animals.
  * Select one or more animals, click Hunt.



### Hunting tips[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Hunting tips")]

  * Animals can be hunted manually instead of just using the hunt order by drafting a colonist and right clicking to fire at animals. This allows the killing of multiple animals in one session, or hunting from close range. Closer range reduces the chance of missing shots or unintended friendly fire, but raises the chance of provoking animal revenge.


  * Wounded animals (either hit with arrows/bullets or cut in particular) tend to bleed. Heavily bleeding animals die after a certain period of time if left untended. Thus it is possible to wound a target and wait until it bleeds to death or drops unconscious due to blood loss. This way you can avoid unnecessary damage to the corpse (and avoid being caught in the explosion left by boomalopes and boomrats). Keep in mind that a wounded but mobile animal can wander away. Unconscious animals can be "saved" - essentially transported to sleeping spots assigned as medical - but without medical treatment they will still die. A valid, if hardly humane, way to hunt.


  * In the occasion of a full horde revenge, early-stage colonies may easily become overrun, leaving all colonists downed. But down is not out. Eventually, one or more of your colonists may recover. With luck, this miracle may take place at nightfall, when wildlife sleep (except for the most enraged beasts among those), or they have wandered far enough, opening a chance to rescue everybody else. The manhunter status of the horde will disappear overnight. 
    * [Melee blocking](/wiki/Defense_tactics#Melee_blocking "Defense tactics") could be a valuable tactic to defeat the rampaging animals. In addition to making it easier to defeat the animals even when outnumbered, it also keeps your downed colonists near your base for easy rescue.



## Life stages[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Life stages")]

Animals all have three different life stages - baby, juvenile and adult. The growth at which they enter the juvenile and adult life stages is determined by each species' **growth time** stat. Animals may have different graphics for different life stages (e.g. [deer](/wiki/Deer "Deer")) or may simply appear smaller. Some animals have a specific name for this stage (e.g. chick or puppy). Animals have different sounds (call, anger, wounded, death) for different life stages, too. Babies may simply make a higher pitched sound or have a different sound altogether (such as chicks). 

The rate at which growth increases is influenced by [saturation](/wiki/Saturation "Saturation"): If saturation is below the first 'hunger mark' ie: hungry, then growth rate is halved. If saturation is below the second 'hunger mark' ie: ravenously hungry, then growth rate is quartered. If saturation is at 0 ie: starving, then growth is completely halted. [Malnutrition](/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition") itself has no impact on growth rate at all. 

Eventually, they reach their final body size and fertility in the final life stage, adulthood. Only upon reaching adulthood can animals produce wool or milk. Eggs may be laid by juveniles.[Verify]

     | Baby Bird | Baby | Juvenile | Adult   
---|---|---|---|---  
**Body Size** | 10% | 20% | 50% | 100%   
**Hunger** | 40% | 40% | 75% | 100%   
**Max Food** | 60% | 60% | 75% | 100%   
**Health Scale** | 25% | 25% | 60% | 100%   
**Market Value** | 40% | 40% | 75% | 100%   
**Move Speed** | 50% | 50% | 90% | 100%   
**Melee Damage** | 50% | 50% | 75% | 100%   
  
The reduction in Body Size of babies and juveniles also affects several other stats, such as [Meat](/wiki/Meat_Amount "Meat Amount") and [Leather Amount](/wiki/Leather_Amount "Leather Amount")

## Needs[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Needs")]

Animals require food and sleep and will fulfil their needs on their own. 

**Food:** Animals will eat any available food according to their diet. Herbivorous animals of the colony can be left to eat grass on their own. Note that animals require different amounts of food compared to humans, as represented by their [Hunger Rate](/wiki/Hunger_Rate "Hunger Rate"). 

**Rest:** Animals will sleep as needed. Tamed animals will sleep in [animal sleeping spots](/wiki/Animal_sleeping_spot "Animal sleeping spot"), [animal sleeping boxes](/wiki/Animal_sleeping_box "Animal sleeping box"), or [animal beds](/wiki/Animal_bed "Animal bed"). If none of which are available, the animal will crash out on the ground inside its allowed zone. A tamed animal will not sleep as long as its master is drafted. 

## Animal husbandry[[edit](/index.php?title=Animals&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Animal husbandry")]

    

Main article: [Animal husbandry](/wiki/Animal_husbandry "Animal husbandry")

Animals can be tamed and put to use in the colony, providing several benefits. Some species can be [trained](/wiki/Animal_husbandry#Training "Animal husbandry") to perform one or possibly more tasks, and will wander about your colony freely. Other species, which can loosely be considered "farm animals", can only be tamed and then put in a [pen](/wiki/Pen "Pen"). This second category can never be trained.
