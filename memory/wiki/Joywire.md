The **joywire** is a brain implant that permanently grants a significant [mood](/wiki/Mood "Mood") boost in return for a substantial [consciousness](/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") penalty. After implantation, it cannot be traditionally removed.

## Acquisition[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Acquisition")]

In the base game, the joywires cannot be crafted. Instead they can only be obtained via [trade](/wiki/Trade "Trade"), as a [quest](/wiki/Quest "Quest") reward or found in [ancient shrines](/wiki/Ancient_shrine "Ancient shrine"). 

With the [Royalty DLC](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Royalty DLC"), Joywires can be crafted at a [machining table](/wiki/Machining_table "Machining table") once the [Brain wiring](/wiki/Research#Brain_wiring "Research") research project has been completed. Note that this research requires a [techprint](/wiki/Techprint "Techprint"). Each requires [![Steel](/images/thumb/9/90/Steel_c.png/16px-Steel_c.png)](/wiki/Steel "Steel") 20 [Steel](/wiki/Steel "Steel"), [![Component](/images/thumb/4/40/Component.png/16px-Component.png)](/wiki/Component "Component") 4 [Components](/wiki/Component "Component"), 15,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4.17 mins) of work modified by the [general labor speed](/wiki/General_Labor_Speed "General Labor Speed") of the crafter, and a [crafting](/wiki/Crafting "Crafting") skill of 5.   
Joywires can be bought from the following traders: [Orbital exotic goods trader](/wiki/Trader/Orbital_Exotic_Goods_Trader "Trader/Orbital Exotic Goods Trader"), [Outlander base trader](/wiki/Trader/Base_Outlander_Standard "Trader/Base Outlander Standard"), [Outlander exotic goods trader](/wiki/Trader/Caravan_Outlander_Exotic "Trader/Caravan Outlander Exotic"), [Slave ship](/wiki/Trader/Orbital_Pirate_Merchant "Trader/Orbital Pirate Merchant"). 

## Summary[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Summary")]

The joywire provides a permanent +30 _Joywire_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") buff at the cost of a −20% [Consciousness](/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") malus. Note that this malus is subtractive, not multiplicative. This consciousness penalty in turn negatively affects almost every task a pawn can perform, as it directly affects the [Eating](/wiki/Eating "Eating"), [Hacking Speed](/wiki/Hacking_Speed "Hacking Speed"), [Manipulation](/wiki/Manipulation "Manipulation"), [Moving](/wiki/Moving "Moving"), [Piloting Ability](/wiki/Piloting_Ability "Piloting Ability"), [Reading Speed](/wiki/Reading_Speed "Reading Speed"), [Talking](/wiki/Talking "Talking") [capacities](/wiki/Capacity "Capacity"), and these in turn affect most [stats](/wiki/Stat "Stat"). See those pages for details. 

### Installation[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Installation")]

Installing the part requires 2,500 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (41.67 secs) of work, 2x medicine of [Herbal](/wiki/Herbal_medicine "Herbal medicine") quality or better, and a [Medical skill](/wiki/Skills#Medical "Skills") of 5. 

If the operation fails, the part is always destroyed. 

Installing a joywire onto a guest is not considered a harmful operation. 

**It cannot be removed traditionally once installed.**

For non-traditional methods, see the Analysis section. 

## Analysis[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Analysis")]

Joywires are most useful when a pawn's productivity simply does not matter. For example, a lategame colony can use a joywire and [psychic harmonizer](/wiki/Psychic_harmonizer "Psychic harmonizer")[![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") to spread a mood buff to other colonists. This works best if the colonist is already disabled, and even better if comatose - see the [Psychic harmonizer](/wiki/Psychic_harmonizer#Comatose_pawns "Psychic harmonizer") page for details. A joywire can also be installed into Royal [quest](/wiki/Quest "Quest") guests[![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") without penalty. [Conceited nobles](/wiki/Conceited_noble "Conceited noble") will not do work, and the implant keeps them happy even if you neglect some of their royal needs. 

Otherwise, implanting a joywire on a colonist is generally a bad idea. The debuff is hefty; even cleaning, hauling, and [walking](/wiki/Move_speed "Move speed") are performed −20% slower. In combat, joywires reduce accuracy and increases the chance to die from consciousness loss. While the +30 mood buff is substantial, increasing mood can be done with many other ways, such as a social [drug](/wiki/Drug "Drug") policy, [fine](/wiki/Fine_meal "Fine meal") or [lavish meals](/wiki/Lavish_meal "Lavish meal"), and beautiful rooms. Joywires could be useful for less useful colonists with [Depressive](/wiki/Depressive "Depressive") or other traits that will cause [mental breaks](/wiki/Mental_break "Mental break"). But, if the other means of mood management aren't sufficient, it may be wiser to just abandon them. 

[Luciferium](/wiki/Luciferium "Luciferium") can offset the stat penalty, bringing Consciousness to 95%. While it's still −20% Consciousness compared to an ordinary luciferium addict, note that certain stats (like Moving) only scale up to 100% Consciousness. Therefore, the penalty is somewhat smaller compared to a non-drugged pawn gaining a joywire. Of course, you should have some means of obtaining luciferium, like a reliable Outlander [trade](/wiki/Trade "Trade") faction. A [neural supercharger](/wiki/Neural_supercharger "Neural supercharger")[![Content added by the Ideology DLC](/images/thumb/9/94/IdeologyIcon.png/16px-IdeologyIcon.png)](/wiki/Ideology_DLC "Content added by the Ideology DLC") is analogous as a stat increase, though doesn't have the same costs as luciferium. 

### Alternatives[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Alternatives")]

The [Very happy](/wiki/Very_happy "Very happy")[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC") and [Happy](/wiki/Happy "Happy")[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC") genes provide much smaller +10 and +5 mood buffs respectively, in exchange for reduced metabolic efficiency, and requiring the appropriate facilities and extracted genes to modify a pawn. The metabolic penalties may be offset or negated entirely by mixing in other genes depending on what is needed or available. 

At the cost of [![Bioferrite](/images/thumb/d/d0/Bioferrite_c.png/16px-Bioferrite_c.png)](/wiki/Bioferrite "Bioferrite") 30 [Bioferrite](/wiki/Bioferrite "Bioferrite"),[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC") a [Bliss lobotomy](/wiki/Bliss_lobotomy "Bliss lobotomy")[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC") provides a smaller +20 mood buff, while reducing [Global Learning Factor](/wiki/Global_Learning_Factor "Global Learning Factor") by −50%, disabling the [Cook](/wiki/Skills#Cooking "Skills"), [Construct](/wiki/Construct "Construct"), [Grow](/wiki/Skills#Plants "Skills"), [Mine](/wiki/Mine "Mine"), [Plant Cut](/wiki/Skills#Plants "Skills"), [Smith, Tailor, Craft](/wiki/Skills#Craft "Skills"), [Research](/wiki/Research "Research") and [Dark Study](/wiki/Research#Anomaly "Research")[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC") work types, and increasing Prison Break Interval by 1000%. This procedure can also be removed with a [healer mech serum](/wiki/Healer_mech_serum "Healer mech serum") or [unnatural healing](/wiki/Unnatural_healing "Unnatural healing").[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC")

Unlike a joywire, these alternatives do not reduce the Consciousness of affected pawns. 

### Removal[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Removal")]

While you cannot traditionally remove the joywire, it is possible to remove it indirectly by destroying the head of the implanted pawn and then resurrecting the pawn using a [resurrector mech serum](/wiki/Resurrector_mech_serum "Resurrector mech serum") or [death refusal](/wiki/Death_refusal "Death refusal").[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC")

This is not without issues however. First, consistently destroying the head can be difficult. With the [Ideology DLC](/wiki/Ideology_DLC "Ideology DLC"), extracting the [skull](/wiki/Skull "Skull") from a dead pawn will do this reliably and safely, but without it, the best way is allow colonists or animals to eat sections of the corpse, at the risk of consuming the entire body and permanently losing the pawn. Furthermore, no matter how successful the head removal, using a mech serum still still runs the risk of [dementia](/wiki/Dementia "Dementia"), [blindness](/wiki/Blindness "Blindness"), and [resurrection psychosis](/wiki/Resurrection_psychosis "Resurrection psychosis"). Death refusal avoids these downsides but has its own constraints. 

## Trivia[[edit](/index.php?title=Joywire&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Trivia")]

The implant cannot be removed in the game, but according to the Joywire Addict and Addiction Counsel [backstories](/wiki/Backstories "Backstories") and the fact the backstories appear on pawns without a joywire installed, this is not the case on other, probably more advanced, worlds.
