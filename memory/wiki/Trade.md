Colonies can trade with other factions by using [caravans](/wiki/Caravan "Caravan") and orbital trade ships via a [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console") and [orbital trade beacon](/wiki/Orbital_trade_beacon "Orbital trade beacon"). Tradeable commodities can include [prisoners](/wiki/Prisoners "Prisoners") and [slaves](/wiki/Slaves "Slaves"),[![Content added by the Ideology DLC](/images/thumb/9/94/IdeologyIcon.png/16px-IdeologyIcon.png)](/wiki/Ideology_DLC "Content added by the Ideology DLC") [animals](/wiki/Animal "Animal"), [furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture"), [gear](/wiki/Gear "Gear"), [resources](/wiki/Resources "Resources"), [food](/wiki/Food "Food"), [drugs](/wiki/Drugs "Drugs"), [works of art](/wiki/Sculptures "Sculptures"), and much more, running almost the entire spectrum of items in RimWorld, depending on the exact trader, what they have in stock to offer and what they are interested in buying from you.

negative = sell` | ONE | ALL   
**[`< <`](/wiki/Controls "Controls")** | **[`<`](/wiki/Controls "Controls")** | `may type # here` | **[`>`](/wiki/Controls "Controls")** | **[`> >`](/wiki/Controls "Controls")**  
  
To initiate a trade, a colonist simply contacts the lead trader. With visiting caravans, this pawn is signified by a yellow question mark "?" above them; select your trader, right-click the visiting trader, and select "Trade", and the selected pawn will march out to meet with the trader. For Orbital traders, who are not physically present on any map (see [below](/wiki/Trade#By_physical_presence "Trade")), similarly, but right click the [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console") and select the party you wish to trade with. When the two come together on the map (your colonist and trader or your colonist and comms console), a Trade menu will pop up. You can make a (partial) trade then, or not, or close this menu and continue with the game, then repeat the process later, as you wish. 

Note that colonists that have "Social" disabled (due to [backstories](/wiki/Backstory "Backstory")) cannot initiate trade nor activate a comms console. 

Actual trade prices are determined by [Trade Price Improvement](/wiki/Trade_Price_Improvement "Trade Price Improvement"), a [stat](/wiki/Stat "Stat") based off the [Social skill](/wiki/Social_skill "Social skill") of the colonist actually doing the trading. Trade Price Improvement is influenced by your trader's ability to [speak](/wiki/Talking "Talking") and [hear](/wiki/Hearing "Hearing"). Damage to any such parts will hurt your trade prices, and you may wish to consider selecting, [training](/wiki/Training "Training"), or otherwise acquiring a different designated trader. 

The trade interface shows what commodities are available to trade - the trader's stock, your colony's current available stock, and the prices to buy and sell. 

  * Items that the foreign trader has no interest in are shown grayed out at the bottom of the list; other traders may want them in the future, but this one does not (see [below](/wiki/Trade#By_inventory "Trade") for more information).
  * Animals that have been downed are not listed for trade.
  * All traders carry a limited amount of [silver](/wiki/Silver "Silver"), so they may not be able to buy everything you wish to sell. You can still give them things over this value but everything they receive will be for free, as they cannot afford to pay. In this instance it is likely better to not sell the items or to take the excess in useful or resalable items rather than get nothing in return.
  * Selling a prisoner is profitable, but has a [negative impact on the happiness](/wiki/Thoughts#A_prisoner_was_sold "Thoughts") of all colonists (excludes [psychopaths](/wiki/Traits "Traits")).
  * Prices listed in green are cheap (does not appear with base-game traders), while prices listed in red are expensive.



  


## Game progression[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Game progression")]

### Early game[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early game")]

Caravans consist of one or more faction members often with [pack animals](/wiki/Pack_animal "Pack animal") and/or tamed [animals](/wiki/Animals "Animals") in tow. Players can either form their own caravan or wait for others to visit their own colony. To trade with a foreign passing caravan, direct a colonist (preferably with high social skill and thus [Trade Price Improvement](/wiki/Trade_Price_Improvement "Trade Price Improvement")) to speak with the caravan member who has a yellow question mark above the head. Stored items will be eligible to sell and appear in the trade window. Purchased items will be dropped in the location where the trader was standing and will need to be hauled. 

Sometimes, foreign caravan members may fight each other and accidentally drop items from their gear inventory. 

### Later game[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Later game")]

After researching the [Microelectronics project](/wiki/Microelectronics "Microelectronics"), a [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console") and an [orbital trade beacon](/wiki/Orbital_trade_beacon "Orbital trade beacon") can be built, which allow: 

  * Satellite calls to request caravans to allied factions at a cost of 15 goodwill (or 700/1100 silver prior to 1.0). You can only call for a caravan from a given friendly faction once every four days.


  * Interaction with orbital trade ships which will stay around for a limited time. A blue notification will appear at the right of your screen to announce the trade ship and its type. After it's gone, a simple notice at the top of the screen will note when the same vessel leaves comms range and can no longer be traded with. Each trade ship varies in type, which determines its inventory.



To trade, order a colonist to interact with the comms console. Only items located within range of an orbital trade beacon can be traded. Purchases will be sent via drop pods to a beacon's open-air tiles, drop pods will phase through constructed roofs otherwise. If all beacon tiles are covered, drop pods will land at the nearest eligible tile. Colonists using the comms console use the same [Trade Price Improvement](/wiki/Trade_Price_Improvement "Trade Price Improvement") stat as when making trades in person. [Prisoners](/wiki/Prisoner "Prisoner") can be sold directly from their cell even if not in range of a beacon and tamed [animals](/wiki/Animals "Animals") can be sold from anywhere on the map. The trade value of a prisoner or animal is based on their capabilities, injuries and skills. 

## Trade prices[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Trade prices")]

Trade prices are based on the [market value](/wiki/Market_value "Market value"). In general the selling price is significantly lower than the buying price. This is however dependent on several modifiers that can be seen in the tooltip when hovering over an item's price in the trading dialog. 

First of all, the player's colony or caravan sells goods at 60% of market value, but buys goods from the trading partner at 140% of market value. These base values are further affected by the both the storyteller's [economic](/wiki/AI_Storytellers#Economy "AI Storytellers") and [difficulty](/wiki/Difficulty "Difficulty") settings. 

Some traders will charge 2x the price for the certain good, in addition to the multiplier on buying. Selling remains at normal price. 

### Trade Price Improvement[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Trade Price Improvement")]

See also: [Trade Price Improvement](/wiki/Trade_Price_Improvement "Trade Price Improvement")

This multiplier is affected by the stats of the chosen colonist. This is listed on the tooltip as `"Your negotiator bonus: -_x_ %"`. Additionally factions offer a 2% discount when trading at a base. 

### Sell Price Multiplier[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Sell Price Multiplier")]

See also: [Sell Price Multiplier](/wiki/Sell_Price_Multiplier "Sell Price Multiplier")

The selling prices of the following item types are affected by this multiplier. Note that this doesn't affect the buying price. 

Type | Factor   
---|---  
[Furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture") | x0.7   
[Sculptures](/wiki/Sculptures "Sculptures") | x1.1   
[Weapons](/wiki/Weapons "Weapons") | x0.2   
[Techprints](/wiki/Techprint "Techprint") [![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") | x0.1   
  
The multipliers make it more difficult to farm raids for profit, because weapons dropped by dead enemies are not valuable in trade. It also means that crafting weapons in your colony only makes sense for personal use, and not for selling. The game encourages creating art for sale, as it is more profitable than all other items. 

## Types of traders[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Types of traders")]

### By technological level[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: By technological level")]

  * **Neolithic** traders are tribal in nature and will be limited to selling neolithic level items.
  * **Outlander** traders come from other faction settlements and are not restricted to a specific technology level of items.
  * **Orbital** traders, or trade ships, can only be seen if you have a powered [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console"). Orbital traders are always "high tech", although certain types (e.g., Farming, or Bulk goods) may offer items that are not particularly distinguishable as such (e.g., cows and corn, and wooden tables and wool jackets, are the same whether the source is neolithic or [glitterworld](/wiki/Glitterworld "Glitterworld")). They can be associated with the [empire](/wiki/Empire "Empire"),[![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") [traders guild](/wiki/Traders_guild "Traders guild"),[![Content added by the Odyssey DLC](/images/thumb/7/7b/OdysseyIcon.png/16px-OdysseyIcon.png)](/wiki/Odyssey_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Odyssey DLC") or with no faction.



### By physical presence[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: By physical presence")]

  * **Faction settlements** that engage in trading. They buy/sell everything within their technology level, have the most extensive stocks and offer a 2% discount.
  * **Land convoys** have a physical presence on the map. They will leave the area if the temperature is deemed too dangerous for them to continue trading. They can come in two types: 
    * **Visitors** are small groups that carry all of their goods on their person. They may or may not have items for trade; even if they do, they often have few items and little silver to trade with.
    * **Trade caravans** are large groups that carry their goods on pack [muffalo](/wiki/Muffalo "Muffalo") or [dromedaries](/wiki/Dromedary "Dromedary"), depending on climate. They have a moderate amount of items and silver to buy trade with.
  * **Orbital traders** have no physical presence. They have large amounts of items and silver to trade with, and have the same trader types as outlanders.

### By inventory[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: By inventory")]

Note that different merchants _tend_ to only accept items of the general category that they offer - so don't necessarily expect to trade your wooden [sculptures](/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture") for firearms, nor your piles of [smokeleaf](/wiki/Smokeleaf "Smokeleaf") for televisions, nor your [herbal medicine](/wiki/Herbal_medicine "Herbal medicine") for [venison](/wiki/Meat "Meat"), nor your valuable [chinchilla](/wiki/Chinchilla "Chinchilla") furs for slaves, depending on exactly what _type_ of trader is offering them. This is not true if you travel to a village site to trade, as then there is no "filter" as to what is offered or accepted beyond the general tech-level of that tribe. 

All traders accept [silver](/wiki/Silver "Silver"), [gold](/wiki/Gold "Gold"), [components](/wiki/Component "Component"), [insect jelly](/wiki/Insect_jelly "Insect jelly"), [art sculpturess](/wiki/Sculptures "Sculptures"),and [packaged survival meals](/wiki/Packaged_survival_meal "Packaged survival meal"), and many will accept [pack animals](/wiki/Pack_animal "Pack animal")*. 

    (* Combat Suppliers and War Merchants are not interested in pack animals. Note that any trader will not trade for an animal if the _current_ temperature is outside that animal's Comfortable Temperature Range (which can be found in the article on that particular animal).)

All traders offer [components](/wiki/Component "Component"). 

#### Bulk goods trader[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Bulk goods trader")]

    Bulk goods traders deal in [food](/wiki/Food "Food") and basic materials, such as [steel](/wiki/Steel "Steel"), [wood](/wiki/Wood "Wood"), [components](/wiki/Component "Component"), [gold](/wiki/Gold "Gold"), and [textiles](/wiki/Textiles "Textiles"), as well as [clothing](/wiki/Clothing "Clothing"), [furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture") and domestic animals.

#### Combat supplier[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Combat supplier")]

    Combat suppliers deal in melee and modern [weapons](/wiki/Weapons "Weapons"), modern [armor](/wiki/Armor "Armor"), [mortar shells](/wiki/Mortar_shell "Mortar shell"), and (non-[herbal](/wiki/Herbal_medicine "Herbal medicine")) [medicine](/wiki/Medicine "Medicine").

#### Exotic goods trader[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Exotic goods trader")]

    Exotic goods traders deal in rare and valuable items such as [artifacts](/wiki/Artifact "Artifact"), [televisions](/wiki/Television "Television"), [telescopes](/wiki/Telescope "Telescope"), [bionic body parts](/wiki/Medical_Items "Medical Items"), [advanced components](/wiki/Advanced_component "Advanced component"), [drugs](/wiki/Drugs "Drugs"), [skilltrainers](/wiki/Skilltrainer "Skilltrainer"), some [furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture"), [plasteel](/wiki/Plasteel "Plasteel"), [gold](/wiki/Gold "Gold"), [uranium](/wiki/Uranium "Uranium"), [glitterworld medicine](/wiki/Glitterworld_medicine "Glitterworld medicine"), and "[psychic" weapons](/wiki/Psychic_Sensitivity#Effects "Psychic Sensitivity"), among others. They may also offer [pack animals](/wiki/Pack_animal "Pack animal") and animals of the wilder variety, or those not native to the local [biome](/wiki/Biomes "Biomes"). They do not accept raw plant material, not even drug precursors.

#### Shaman merchant[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Shaman merchant")]

    The tribal variant of the exotic goods trader, the shaman merchant deals in lower tech versions of the same, including [smokeleaf](/wiki/Smokeleaf "Smokeleaf"). They also may offer high-tech medicines and "[psychic" weapons](/wiki/Psychic_Sensitivity#Effects "Psychic Sensitivity").

#### War merchant[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: War merchant")]

    The tribal version of the Combat Supplier, this merchant offers simple melee weapons and [neolithic ranged weapons](/wiki/Weapon_Guide "Weapon Guide"), predatory-type animals, and tribal-level (non-[complex clothing](/wiki/Research#Medieval_Research_Projects "Research")) apparel of all sorts.

## Trader arrivals[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Trader arrivals")]

Trader arrivals are handled differently depending on the storyteller and whether the trader is a caravan, a royal tribute collector or an orbital trader. Each player settlement has its own arrival calculations, so having multiple settlements creates additional opportunities for traders. 

### Trade caravans[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Trade caravans")]

Cassandra and Phoebe generate five possible trade caravan dates during each 60-day year. These dates are determined by the game seed and the settlement, rather than being rolled randomly each day. 

The following restrictions apply: 

  * Trade caravans cannot be generated during the first five days after initially settling.
  * Possible dates for the same settlement are separated by at least six days.
  * Each settlement has its own possible dates.
  * A newly created settlement joins the current yearly cycle. Its dates are not calculated from the day that map was created.
  * A possible date is lost if the caravan cannot be generated or placed.



A faction-relations check determines which of the five possible visits can proceed. The check counts factions that are currently neutral or allied and compares them with all factions that are capable of becoming non-hostile. Permanently hostile factions are not included. 

In a standard game with six main factions, two are permanently hostile and the other four are included in this calculation: 

Neutral or allied factions | Visit chance | Average accepted dates out of five   
---|---|---  
0 of 4 | 0% | 0   
1 of 4 | 60% | 3   
2 of 4 | 73.3% | 3.67   
3 of 4 | 86.7% | 4.33   
4 of 4 | 100% | 5   
  
These figures describe the faction-relations check. Passing this check does not guarantee an arrival. The game must still find a non-hostile faction that can currently provide a valid trade caravan and successfully generate and place the group. For example, the traders guild provides orbital traders but cannot send a ground caravan. 

The five possible dates are predetermined, but the faction-relations check uses relations at the time each date is reached. Improving relations before a possible date can increase the chance that it produces a caravan. It does not create any additional possible dates. 

#### Visit chance calculation[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Visit chance calculation")]

The initial faction proportion is: 

    Neutral or allied factions ÷ factions capable of becoming non-hostile

If there are no neutral or allied factions, the visit chance is 0%. Otherwise, a proportion of 25% or less gives a 60% visit chance, while a proportion of 100% gives a 100% visit chance. Values between these points are scaled evenly. 

For proportions between 25% and 100%, the calculation is: 

    Visit chance = 60% + ((faction proportion − 25%) ÷ 75%) × 40%

For example, if two of the four eligible factions are neutral or allied, the faction proportion is 50%: 

    60% + ((50% − 25%) ÷ 75%) × 40% = 73.3%

### Royal tribute collectors[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Royal tribute collectors")]

With [Royalty](/wiki/Royalty "Royalty") active, royal tribute collectors use a similar seeded schedule. Each player home map has two candidate tribute collector dates per 60-day year, separated by at least 30 days. 

Whether a candidate date is accepted depends on the number of non-hostile factions at the time of the check. The map must have at least 20,000 [wealth](/wiki/Wealth "Wealth"), and at least 20 days must have passed since the player first settled. The Empire must be non-hostile and able to send the group. A newly created settlement joins its own seeded schedule at the current date; it does not wait an additional 20 days based on that map's age. 

Other non-hostile factions  | Visit chance  | Average accepted dates per year   
---|---|---  
4 of 4  | 100%  | 2.00   
3 of 4  | 89.3%  | 1.79   
2 of 4  | 78.7%  | 1.57   
1 of 4  | 68.0%  | 1.36   
0 of 4  | 60.0%  | 1.20   
  
This assumes the Empire is non-hostile and the standard Royalty faction set: the Empire, four other factions capable of becoming non-hostile, and three permanently hostile factions. Map conditions like Unnatural darkness, Blood rain, Lava flow or a temperature outside of −40°C to 45°C all stop a scheduled visit happening. 

### Orbital traders[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Orbital traders")]

Orbital traders use a separate cycle under Cassandra Classic and Phoebe Chillax. Each settlement alternates between: 

  * A seven-day active period containing one possible orbital trader arrival.
  * An eight-day inactive period with no possible arrival.



This creates one possible orbital trader arrival every 15 days, or four per 60-day year. Orbital traders do not use the faction-relations visit chance applied to trade caravans. 

An arrival still requires a valid orbital trader type and colonists on the map. No more than five passing ships of any kind can be present simultaneously. 

A powered [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console") is needed to receive the arrival message and contact an orbital trader. A powered [orbital trade beacon](/wiki/Orbital_trade_beacon "Orbital trade beacon") is needed to trade physical items within its range. Neither building is required for the ship itself to arrive. Orbital traders remain available for 40,000 ticks, or 16 in-game hours. 

### Randy[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Randy")]

[Randy Random](/wiki/Randy_Random "Randy Random") does not use the fixed trade caravan or orbital trader cycles. These arrivals are selected from his random incident categories, so their future dates cannot be calculated in the same way. 

Royal tribute collectors are an exception. Their dedicated schedule remains active when Randy is the storyteller.

### Orbital traders[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Orbital traders")]

In order to trade with an Orbital trader, you first must be able to make contact with them. This requires 3 things: 

  1. a powered [comms console](/wiki/Comms_console "Comms console")
  2. a powered [orbital trade beacon](/wiki/Orbital_trade_beacon "Orbital trade beacon")
  3. an Orbital Trader currently in the area - they are not always up there.



If you have the first two, you will be automatically notified when the third appears. The trader leaves in 16 hours, or 40,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (11.11 mins), after arrival. 

Orbital traders usually come with large stocks corresponding to their trader type. You cannot "request" a specific type of orbital trader the way you can when contacting allies. 

Note that you can only trade items within the trade beacon's range, with the exception of living creatures.

## Trader behavior[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Trader behavior")]

Trade caravans enter from the edge of the map and travel to a camping spot near the colony. The game first searches for a suitable outdoor location near certain colony buildings, including campfires. If it cannot find one, it broadens the search to other colony buildings and then to colonists or prisoners. 

The selected cell must be standable, outdoors, connected to the map edge and inside an outdoor area containing at least 60 cells. The game also favors open locations that can reach the colony and have several colony buildings nearby and visible. These requirements do not consider whether the location is convenient, comfortable or dry, so traders may camp in places such as shallow rivers. 

The caravan only arrives if all generated members can reach the selected camping spot. After reaching it, its members behave according to their roles: 

  * The trader, identified by a yellow question mark, remains near the camping spot.
  * Guards defend and wander within approximately 28 cells of the camping spot.
  * Pack animals carrying inventory follow close to the trader.
  * Animals and slaves offered for sale escort the trader and may help defend them when enemies approach.



Caravan members satisfy ordinary needs during their visit. They may use colony tables and chairs when eating, and exhausted members may sleep on the ground. Humanlike members can also socialize and may start social fights among themselves. 

Members automatically respond to hostile pawns and animals. They may fight raiders, hostile factions, manhunting animals or other threats without the player's involvement. The game avoids initially sending a neutral caravan when a hostile group already blocks its arrival, but a caravan already on the map can encounter groups that arrive later. 

When any member is harmed, the caravan temporarily changes to a defensive state centered on the trader's current position. If the trader is unavailable, it defends around a pack animal or another caravan member. After 1,200 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") without further harm, the caravan resumes traveling to its camping spot or returns to its normal camping behavior. 

A downed caravan member may be rescued by another member of the caravan or by the player. Tending a non-hostile faction member and allowing them to leave the map can grant [goodwill](/wiki/Goodwill "Goodwill"). Each player tend can provide one goodwill, up to ten tends. If the pawn leaves without significant bleeding and is not in a mental state, another 12 goodwill can be awarded. 

If the tended pawn is the faction's actual leader, leaving the map healthy and not in a mental state grants an additional 40 goodwill. This can provide 52 goodwill before goodwill from tending is added, or as much as 62 goodwill in total. 

Items dropped by caravan members can be unforbidden and collected without directly affecting relations. Stripping a living member of a non-hostile faction causes a loss of 40 goodwill. 

A caravan member being killed, downed or imprisoned counts as a violent loss. Any such loss prevents the caravan from leaving a gift. Losing the trader or any pack animal makes the group leave immediately, while losing at least 20% of all caravan members causes the entire group to flee while fighting. 

### Leaving the map[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Leaving the map")]

[![StumpChoppedA \(cropped\).png](/images/thumb/6/6a/StumpChoppedA_%28cropped%29.png/40px-StumpChoppedA_%28cropped%29.png)](/wiki/Category:Stubs "Category:Stubs") | **This section is a[stub](/wiki/Category:Stubs "Category:Stubs").** You can help RimWorld Wiki by **[expanding it](https://rimworldwiki.com/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit). After reaching their camping spot, traders remain there for between 27,000 and 45,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time"), or 10.8 to 18 in-game hours. 

#### Gifts[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: Gifts")]

When this period ends, the caravan may leave a gift near one of its members. A gift is only considered if the caravan completed its normal visit and none of its members, including animals, were killed, downed or imprisoned. Dismissing the caravan or causing it to leave early prevents the gift check. 

The chance of receiving a gift is: 

    25% × colony wealth factor × faction goodwill factor

The wealth factor is 100% at 30,000 colony wealth or less. It falls to 10% at 80,000 wealth and reaches 0% at 300,000 wealth. Values between these points are scaled evenly. 

The goodwill factor is 100% at 0 goodwill or higher and 0% at −30 goodwill or lower. Values between −30 and 0 are scaled evenly. Hostile factions always have a 0% gift chance. 

For a faction with at least 0 goodwill, the resulting chances include: 

Colony wealth | Gift chance   
---|---  
30,000 or less | 25%   
50,000 | 16%   
80,000 | 2.5%   
300,000 or more | 0%   
  
If the gift check succeeds, the game selects one of five gift categories: 

Gift category | Selection chance   
---|---  
[Silver](/wiki/Silver "Silver") | 16.7%   
Raw resources worth at least 6 silver each | 20.8%   
Industrial medicine, glitterworld medicine, components or advanced components | 20.8%   
Apparel | 20.8%   
Weapons | 20.8%   
  
Some reward-tagged apparel and weapons are excluded, as is the smokepop belt. 

The gift has a base market value between 100 and 500 silver. This is multiplied by a second colony wealth factor. At 10,000 wealth or less, the resulting range is approximately 25 to 125 silver. It increases with wealth until reaching the full 100 to 500 silver range at 100,000 wealth. 

The gift is generated when the check succeeds. It is not taken from the caravan's existing trade inventory and does not represent goods that would otherwise be dropped if its members were killed. Depending on the generated contents, a gift may contain a stack or several separate items. 

A [letter](/wiki/Letter "Letter") titled "Gift from <faction>" appears, and the generated items are placed near one of the caravan members. Greater colony wealth therefore makes gifts less likely, while increasing their potential value. 

#### Departure[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Departure")]

The trader moves toward the edge of the map, with the rest of the caravan following or escorting its members. Trading remains possible while the trader is leaving, although catching up may be difficult. Caravan members leave the map individually upon reaching its edge. 

A trader can be dismissed early. Select a colonist capable of [Social](/wiki/Social "Social"), right-click the trader marked with a yellow question mark and select "Dismiss trader". A dismissed caravan does not receive a gift check. 

Merely harming a caravan member does not make the caravan leave immediately. The group defends itself and may resume its visit after 1,200 ticks without further harm. However, the caravan leaves early if any of the following occurs: 

  * A caravan member develops heatstroke or hypothermia above 15% severity.
  * With [Anomaly](/wiki/Anomaly "Anomaly") active, a caravan member's blood rage exceeds 15% severity during [blood rain](/wiki/Blood_rain "Blood rain").
  * A caravan member cannot reach the map edge. The group changes to an exit behavior that permits it to dig through obstructions.
  * The caravan's faction becomes hostile to the player.
  * The trader is killed, downed or imprisoned.
  * A [pack animal](/wiki/Pack_animal "Pack animal") is killed, downed or imprisoned, even if it was not carrying goods.
  * At least 20% of the entire group, including animals, is killed, downed or imprisoned.
  * With [Odyssey](/wiki/Odyssey "Odyssey") active, a lava flow begins.



Caravan guards may temporarily remain behind to defend a downed carrier or the corpse of a pack animal while an active hostile threat is nearby. Once the threat is gone, they resume leaving. If the escorted departure has not finished after 60,000 ticks, or one in-game day, the remaining members switch to a direct exit behavior.

### Tame wild animals right before trading[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Tame wild animals right before trading")]

If you have a very good tamer such as one with [animal personhood](/wiki/Animal_personhood "Animal personhood") meme and many wild animals in your map. Try to tame the ones with high market value and low wildness and sell them immediately. 

To reduce the distance your tamer has to travel, form a caravan and force them to pick up all the food they need and don't let them lead the tamed animal back to your pen. Some easy animals to tame are [alpaca](/wiki/Alpaca "Alpaca"), [donkey](/wiki/Donkey "Donkey"), [horse](/wiki/Horse "Horse"), [muffalo](/wiki/Muffalo "Muffalo")

## Notes[[edit](/index.php?title=Trade&action=edit&section=38 "Edit section: Notes")]

  * There is no trade bar for "silver"; that's just listed at the top of the trades column, and gets adjusted as items are selected to buy/sell. Once a trade is accepted, the difference in silver (positive or negative) automatically goes from one party to the other, up to the limit of that party's silver in stock. (Trader's silver can be seen under their "Gear" tab, your silver can be seen under Inventory/Raw Resources.)
  * You cannot buy back prisoners you just sold as a way to insta-recruit anyone (exploit prevention).
  * You can trade your expensive items (weapons, sculptures) for other desirable items if the trade ship doesn't have enough silver to complete the transaction. Of course, the amount of silver you can gain is lower, but it is usually better to have a lot of cheap items than an expensive one in your store. Sometimes you have items so expensive that no trader can buy them.
  * When selling pack animals, they will bring any items that they are carrying along with them.
