As its name suggests, this is a quick guide for starting **quickly** - when you want to play
**now**. Detailed analysis, tips and tricks, and "best practices" will be skipped in this guide,
excepting only to avoid "game over" level pitfalls.

For a more detailed basic introduction see [Basics][18].

## Setup[[edit][19]]

### Choosing a scenario[[edit][20]]

The first thing you'll be able to pick is a story [scenario][21]. There are 4 prebuilt scenarios to
choose from. This guide assumes that you're choosing the classic ["Crashlanded"][22] scenario; it's
a good "first game" start, and many veteran players prefer it.

### Choosing a storyteller[[edit][23]]

You can pick one of the 3 [AI Storytellers][24], and a difficulty level. The storytellers only
determine the random events that occur during your game. The difficulty and storyteller can be
changed at any time by going into the Options, selecting the Gameplay tab, and click "Modify
Storyteller".

It is recommended to choose **[Cassandra Classic][25]** on **[Strive to Survive][26]** to get a feel
for how the game is designed to play out. However, **there is no shame** in picking an easier
difficulty. Play Peaceful if you want to get a hang of how the UI works and how to control your
colonists.

### Creating a world[[edit][27]]

You can play with the seed* and map size of the world, but it isn't anything that will make a big
difference to you yet. The default dimensions is a good size that won't cause too much lag.

  (* The "seed" is just a random word, lower/upper case sensitive. Write it down for reference if
  you might want to duplicate your map later, once you know the game better.)

Once the world is created, pick a [temperate forest][28] biome to start, hopefully not too far from
friendly(ish) tribes - the yellows and purples. Bonus points for a river/creek, but hardly
necessary.

Now, look in the info tab (lower left on the screen). A map with a "year-round" growing season is
the easiest, but you don't want to be too hot. Try between latitude 20 and 30 or so - too far south,
near the equator, will be brutally hot very soon (summer), and too far north will have unforgiving
winters.

You will also want to pay attention to the terrain type. Terrain goes from Flat, Small Hills, Large
Hills, and Mountain. The bigger the hills, the more [ore][29] and natural defenses are available,
but the less open space there is to build and grow. Pick either Small Hills or Large Hills; they are
a nice balance between minerals, defense, and growing space.

### Choosing your characters[[edit][30]]

This can take some time, but with only 3 starting characters, you may want to get a good starting
trio. You can click randomize on your colonists as many times as you want, without penalty. You can
also move them down to "save" them while you randomize a different character, if you find one you're
not sure about.

**Skills**
You will want at least 1 colonist with a **passion** (one/two flames) and **5** or more levels for
the following skills:
* **Shooting** - Defending your colony
* **Plants** - Feeding your colony
* **Construction** - Building your colony
* **Medical** - Healing injuries and disease, both of which are inevitable.

Rolling for [passions][31] (one or two flames) in a skill is just as important as rolling for a high
skill, because they will level up faster.

Having a colonist skilled in each of **Cooking** (cooking food), **Social** (recruiting
[prisoners][32], trade), **Mining** (harvesting [ore][33]), and **Intellectual** (faster research)
is also useful to have. You don't need every single one of these skills, but having most/all of
these covered can hasten your advancement in the early game.

  (That leaves 4 skills not covered; if you get them, great! But none are needed at the start, nor
  should be worried about for a quick start...
  
    * Melee: How to club someone/something with your gun when you missed too many times. Can be
      brutal.
    * Animals: Taming, training, and hunting. For food, you can rely on plants for the entire game -
      it's much less hassle.
    * Crafting: Making high quality gear, clothing, weapons. "good enough" is good enough to start.
    * Art: Beauty can wait until survival is covered.)

Remember, your [characters][34] can only do one thing at a time, and they need time to eat,
recreate, and sleep. While skills like Shooting and Medical won't be used all the time, remember
that a full-time Planter can't be a full-time Cook. In this type of situation, you don't need to
abandon the colonist good at 2 things; instead, you should grab another colonist that is also good
at Plants and/or Cooking.

**Traits**
RimWorld is designed around non-perfect colonists. Good [traits][35] don't matter too much - whilst
helpful, the game is playable without them. However, there are some very bad traits that should be
avoided.
* [Lazy][36] / [Slothful][37] / [Slowpoke][38] (works more slowly)
* [Pessimist][39] / [Depressive][40] (permanent mood penalty)
* [Chemical fascination][41] / [Chemical Interest][42] (likely to become addicted to anything you
  have lying around)
* [Pyromaniac][43] (you are starting with wooden walls... do I have to explain this one?)

**Other Factors**
Try not to accept characters who have a lot of health ailments, old painful wounds, or are addicted
to drugs. A bad heart can be a killer, of that character and the colony that depended on them. Look
under the Health tab to see how problems affect their overall Movement, Consciousness, Manipulation,
etc.

Lastly, be conscious of potential problems with characters "[Incapable of][44]..." Dumb Labor
(hauling, cleaning), Firefighting, or Violence (both hunting and self-defense). "Incapable of
Skilled Labor" should be an immediate rejection. Have at least 2 colonists able to do Dumb Labor and
Violence.

That said, some combinations of {good traits + skills} can balance out otherwise unacceptable bad
traits. If you have a great Researcher, who has piles of movement penalties and no violence - well,
he's not going to be moving very much while sitting at his desk doing research, is he? No character
is perfect, find ones you can live with, and a group of 3 that covers most of the bases.

## Getting off on the right foot[[edit][45]]

### Landing[[edit][46]]

Allow everything. Right click "Allow". (Architect -> Orders tab)
* **PAUSE THE GAME**! - Wait for the colonists to emerge from their pods, then press spacebar.
  (Note: "Pause on load" can be set to default in the Options.)
* **Unforbid Everything!** At the start, every item is "Forbidden", prohibiting your colonists from
  using them. The easiest way to unforbid everything on the map (at any time) is to simply right
  click the 'Allow' order and allowing all. You can also un/forbid items, one or a few at a time, by
  selecting them and hitting the [f] key, or you can open the Architect/Orders tab, select "Allow",
  and left-click-hold and drag an area across your entire map.
* **Arm Yourselves**: You'll start with a [bolt-action rifle][47], a [revolver][48], and a plasteel
  [knife][49]. Select the pawn with the best Shooting skill and then right-click the bolt-action
  rifle and select Equip. Equip the next best shooter with a pistol, then equip the knife on the
  last pawn or the brawler.

  (You can decide which person to wear the armor. The flak pants and vest each slow down movement
  slightly, which is not a great thing, and you probably(?) don't need protection at this point. Put
  the helmet on someone, that doesn't hurt.)
Example work priorities
* **Prioritize**: Go into your "Work" tab, and turn on manual priorities.
  * Firefight, Patient, Bed Rest, and Basic, should be set to priority 1 on everyone.
  * Doctor, Warden, Cook, Construct, and Grow should be set to priority 1 on the colonist with the
    highest skill and disabled for everyone else.
  * Any passions or interests should be set to priority 2, then 4 for every other work.
    Even if they have poor Intellectual, a colonist is better off researching than being idle - you
    can't fail research.
  * Temporarily set Hauling to 1 until they are done hauling sensitive equipment (explained later).
* **Recon**: Take a look around and get a feel for the terrain. Where are natural choke points to
  create kill zones? Where are standing structures that you can make use of?
"Reconstructing" a ruin can save time.
* **Prepare**: Decide on a site for your initial base camp. Find a spot on the map relatively close
  to the landing site where you feel you can set up in a reasonably short period of time. If there
  are abandoned buildings or a hill nearby, consider taking advantage of them by building against
  them. Then set up a chop wood order to gather more wood.

  You need to get a single [wooden][50] building up as soon as possible so your colonists can sleep
  under a roof, and you can haul materials inside to stop them from deteriorating. Don't use
  [steel][51] if avoidable* - trees are plentiful in the suggested biome and steel is a valuable
  resource throughout the entire game, too valuable for walls (for now).Even if you plan on digging
  a mountain base, throwing up some walls is much faster to get settled in quickly.

  Go into the [Architect][52] menu to start to get familiar with it. You can change the material of
  a building by clicking on it in the menu before you place it.

### Your first day[[edit][53]]

Build a wooden room 5x7 or 5x9. Build [walls][54] around it, a [door][55], and a wooden [light][56].
Build [stockpile zones][57] inside: one for food, one for weapons/apparel, one for building
materials (wood/steel). Unforbid items and they will be hauled into the stockpiles.

Build a [campfire][58] (or heater if you have the research) - place it in the middle of the room
or just outside the door if it's hot outside. Keep it at target temperature 21°C (70°F) if cold.

Build a [simple research bench][59] and start [research][60]. Good early research projects include
[passive cooler][61] (if hot), [stonecutting][62], [complex clothing][63], [beds][64], and
[electricity][65] (especially [stone walls][66] and [doors][67]).

Build a [campfire stove][68] or cooking stove to start cooking [simple meals][69] - better yet,
[hunt][70] some animals for meat. Make at least 10 simple meals, but don't worry about variety yet.

Plant some crops in a [growing zone][71]. Rice is fast but requires fertile soil, corn is slow but
stores well. Potatoes are a good middle ground. Plant enough to feed your colonists indefinitely.

### Your first night[[edit][72]]

Make sure everyone is assigned a [bed][73] (you can use sleeping spots temporarily). Ensure the
campfire/heater is working. Your colonists should have food available. Prioritize building if
temperatures are dangerous.

**Tips**: If it's very cold, prioritize building a heater or more campfires. If you're starving,
prioritize hunting and cooking over construction.

## The next few days[[edit][74]]

Continue expanding your base. Build [batteries][75] once you have electricity research to store
power. Build [coolers][76] if it's hot (as passive coolers or for food preservation). Build a
[freezer][77] room to prevent food from spoiling.

Expand your [growing zones][78] and ensure you have enough food for winter (if applicable). Start
[trading][79] with caravans or orbital traders when they arrive.

Build some basic defenses - [sandbags][80] or a simple wall with a [turret][81] behind it.

## Your first battle[[edit][82]]

When a raid arrives, draft your colonists and position them behind cover (sandbags, walls, or
natural terrain). Target the closest enemies first. Don't let your colonists get surrounded.

If someone goes down, rescue them immediately and get them to a [medical bed][83]. Prioritize
treating the most critical injuries.

## Moving forward[[edit][84]]

### Your first winter[[edit][85]]

If you're in a biome with winter, you need:
* Stored food (corn or packaged survival meals work well)
* Warm clothing ([parkas][86] or [tuques][87])
* Heated indoor areas
* Enough wood/coal/power to last the winter

Winter is slow but survivable with preparation. Animals will migrate or die, so hunting becomes
harder. Focus on indoor work like research, crafting, and construction.

## Other tips moving forward[[edit][88]]

* Don't neglect [joy][89] - colonists with low recreation will have mental breaks
* Keep your colony clean - filth causes disease and lowers mood
* Build a [dining room][90] and [rec room][91] to keep mood high
* Start [mining][92] for steel and components
* Trade regularly to get resources you don't have
* Keep an eye on [mood][93] - mental breaks can be devastating
