The **battery** is a [building](/wiki/Building "Building") that stores excess [power](/wiki/Power "Power") from a grid to use when needed.

## Acquisition[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Acquisition")]

Batteries can be constructed once the [Battery](/wiki/Research#Battery "Research") research project has been completed. Each requires [![Steel](/images/thumb/9/90/Steel_c.png/16px-Steel_c.png)](/wiki/Steel "Steel") 70 [Steel](/wiki/Steel "Steel"), [![Component](/images/thumb/4/40/Component.png/16px-Component.png)](/wiki/Component "Component") 2 [Components](/wiki/Component "Component") and 800 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (13.33 secs) of work modified by the [construction speed](/wiki/Construction_speed "Construction speed") of the builder.   
Batteries can not be bought from traders. 

## Summary[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Summary")]

[![](/images/thumb/8/82/Zero_conduit_freezer.jpg/250px-Zero_conduit_freezer.jpg)](/wiki/File:Zero_conduit_freezer.jpg)

[](/wiki/File:Zero_conduit_freezer.jpg "Enlarge")

Batteries connect directly to the wind turbine and the attached coolers, with no conduits. This power grid will only "Zzztt" if the batteries get rained on.

Batteries automatically charge from the excess power in a power grid and discharge to power other buildings connected to the grid when there is a power deficit. However, batteries have a 50% efficiency, meaning they take in only half of a grid's excess power. For example, an excess of 200 W for 24 hours will only store 100 Wd. Power is evenly divided between all batteries connected to the grid in question. Each battery is initially constructed empty but can store up to 600 Wd (Watt-days) of [power](/wiki/Power "Power") at once. 1 Watt-day is equal to powering a 1 W appliance for 24 hours. There is no need to enable or "switch" to battery power - a charged battery will automatically discharge as soon as there is a power deficit in the grid to which it is connected. There is no limit or penalty for power output. 

Each tile of battery will also transfer an infinite amount of power through it, as if it were a [power conduit](/wiki/Power_conduit "Power conduit"). Therefore, electric appliances like [standing lamps](/wiki/Standing_lamp "Standing lamp") can connect within 6 tiles of a battery. The battery can't connect in the same way; it requires an adjacent conduit, battery, or power generator to power. A wire will appear when a valid connection is made; you may need to use the Reconnect gizmo on the appliance for it to be powered. 

Batteries lose 5 Wd of stored power per day, even if they aren't connected to anything or are uninstalled in their minified state. Batteries can't be turned off, but a [power switch](/wiki/Power_switch "Power switch") can disconnect them from other buildings. 

Batteries are temporarily disabled by sources of [EMP](/wiki/EMP "EMP"), with downtime scaling to the EMP damage dealt.[Verify] During this time, it will not provide any power. Unlike mechanoids, batteries do not adapt to EMP and thus can be continuously restunned. 

### Health[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Health")]

An installed battery will [short circuit](/wiki/Short_circuit "Short circuit") if left in the [rain](/wiki/Rain "Rain") or [snow](/wiki/Snow "Snow") without a [roof](/wiki/Roof "Roof"). In addition, the total power stored in batteries contributes the effect of any short circuit that affects that power grid. 

Like other [furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture"), batteries are not subject to [deterioration](/wiki/Deterioration "Deterioration"), but short-circuits from rain or snow cause damage.[Verify/Detail] Their health does not impact power storage, discharge, or any other function. [Temperature](/wiki/Temperature "Temperature") also has no effect past [fire](/wiki/Fire "Fire") creation. However, batteries can explode when reaching low enough health.[Detail]

## Analysis[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Analysis")]

Batteries are most useful when using [wind turbines](/wiki/Wind_turbine "Wind turbine") or [solar panels](/wiki/Solar_panel "Solar panel"), both of which are highly variable power sources. Using a battery creates a 'cushion' in case no power is generated, thus making their power more reliable. Batteries are required to use any sort of solar power at night or during an [eclipse](/wiki/Eclipse "Eclipse"). Fueled generators can also benefit, as excess power can be stored and remain usable after the generator is turned off. 

Even in colonies that do not use wind or solar energy, batteries are useful to cover spikes in power usage, such as short-circuits, [turret](/wiki/Turret "Turret") arrays, or [electric smelters](/wiki/Electric_smelter "Electric smelter"). A battery can cover arbitrarily high power needs, for a length of time dependent on the sudden power requirement. As an example, an array of 20 [mini-turrets](/wiki/Mini-turret "Mini-turret") and 4 [autocannon turrets](/wiki/Autocannon_turret "Autocannon turret") draws 3200 W of power. Instead of building another [geothermal generator](/wiki/Geothermal_generator "Geothermal generator"), each battery will power this for more than 4 in-game hours. 

Uninstalled batteries can be used whenever power needs to be transported. They can be used to power remote [deep drills](/wiki/Deep_drill "Deep drill"), or even be put on a [caravan](/wiki/Caravan "Caravan"). This allows for electric appliances like [mini-turrets](/wiki/Mini-turret "Mini-turret") to be used far away from your base. 

As there is no limit on charge or discharge rate and power is divided evenly between batteries, the minimum number of batteries should be used to achieve the required storage. For every 600Wd is routinely left unfilled that is an additional 5W spent, components consumed by [breakdowns](/wiki/Breakdown "Breakdown"), and [![Steel](/images/thumb/9/90/Steel_c.png/16px-Steel_c.png)](/wiki/Steel "Steel") 70 [Steel](/wiki/Steel "Steel"), [![Component](/images/thumb/4/40/Component.png/16px-Component.png)](/wiki/Component "Component") 2 [Components](/wiki/Component "Component") spent all for no benefit. 

### Power storage[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Power storage")]

Separating your batteries behind stone [walls](/wiki/Wall "Wall") will help keep potential fires from spreading. As you'll rarely need to access the battery itself, stone doors can be safely used for "battery rooms". You may want to keep some additional batteries behind a [power switch](/wiki/Power_switch "Power switch") on a separate network, so they can be brought online in the case of a "Zzzt..." event. Alternatively, you can uninstall batteries and keep them in a warehouse. Either way, the discharge is negligible. 

When powering an emergency array, the battery group should be isolated. There should be 1 power switch to the main power grid, which should be turned on or off when needed. This keeps power safe from a short circuit. When powering a security array, you can place another, separate switch connected to your turrets to easily power them on or off. 

Unused electric work benches and powered appliances can be turned off to reduce total power usage, via the Designate toggle power order in the work bench menu. This requires a pawn to travel to the appliance and manually turn it off or on. A faster alternative to the Designate Toggle Power order is to build a single piece of [power conduit](/wiki/Power_conduit "Power conduit") unattached to any power source near the work bench. The Reconnect [order](/wiki/Order "Order") from the work bench menu will instantly toggle power connection between the unpowered, solo conduit and your main power grid, saving power usage without requiring work or travel from your pawns. 

### Efficiency[[edit](/index.php?title=Battery&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Efficiency")]

When calculating power capacity, keep in mind that charge efficiency is only 50%; half of the excess energy is lost. This is particularly relevant when planning a [sun lamp](/wiki/Sun_lamp "Sun lamp") setup. The lamp draws 2900 W during the day, and 0 W during the night, for an average demand of 1595 W. However, an average power of 1595 W is not sufficient, and only enough to power the lamp for 6 hours. Simply adding more batteries will not change this; the problem is the lack of energy. In this scenario, adding another [solar generator](/wiki/Solar_generator "Solar generator") would be enough to power the lamp.
