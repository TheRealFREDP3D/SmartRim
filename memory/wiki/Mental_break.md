A **mental break** is a pawn event that may occur when a pawns's **mood** drops below the **mental break threshold**. Majority types of mental break cause a **mental state** , during which the pawn will become uncontrollable and shirk work tasks for a short while, or even worse, they will attempt to sabotage the colony. A breaking colonist may be interrupted by arresting or downing them. 

A few types of mental break, like catatonic breakdown, run wild, and crisis of belief,[![Content added by the Ideology DLC](/images/thumb/9/94/IdeologyIcon.png/16px-IdeologyIcon.png)](/wiki/Ideology_DLC "Content added by the Ideology DLC") do not cause mental state. Some mental states may occur without a mental break, notable examples of sources are [psychic insanity lance](/wiki/Psychic_insanity_lance "Psychic insanity lance"), [pyromaniac](/wiki/Pyromaniac "Pyromaniac") trait, [gourmand](/wiki/Gourmand "Gourmand") trait, [Alzheimer's](/wiki/Alzheimer%27s "Alzheimer's"), [dementia](/wiki/Dementia "Dementia"), [drug](/wiki/Drug "Drug") withdrawal, and various [psycasts](/wiki/Psycast "Psycast"),[![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") some of which enable the player to induce mental states in enemies in battle. 

There are three levels of severity for mental break events: **minor, major,** and **extreme**. Each severity level has a different pool of possible events that may occur at different frequencies. Minor events include hiding in the pawn's room for a day, extreme events include murder. 

Colonists, visitors, enemies, tamed, and wild animals all have types of mental breaks. 

## Mechanics[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Mechanics")]

### Mood[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Mood")]

[Mood](/wiki/Mood "Mood") is a visual indicator of how likely your pawn is to have a mental break. It is lowered by negative [thoughts](/wiki/Thoughts "Thoughts") about the pawn's environment and raised by positive thoughts. Mood is found in the [Needs](/wiki/Needs "Needs") tab of the pawn Inspect pane. 

The three mental break severity levels directly correspond to the low mood levels **Stressed, On edge,** and **About to break,** respectively. Mental break events and frequencies are different for each mood level. 

Pawn mood is related to the sum of all the thoughts a pawn has, so minor penalties can quickly add up to large effects on a pawn's stability. Some mental breaks are unavoidable in even the finest, happiest colonies, such as **Fire Starting Sprees** by pyromaniac pawns. 

### Thresholds[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Thresholds")]

Main article: [Mental Break Threshold](/wiki/Mental_Break_Threshold "Mental Break Threshold")

A specific pawn's mental break thresholds are affected by various personality [traits](/wiki/Traits "Traits"). Traits may directly raise or lower mental break threshold, such as [Steadfast](/wiki/Steadfast "Steadfast") or [Nervous](/wiki/Nervous "Nervous") respectively. Some traits add permanent negative thoughts, like [Pessimist](/wiki/Pessimist "Pessimist"), or conditional negative thoughts, like [Night owl](/wiki/Night_owl "Night owl"). Multiple traits have an additive effect, but the minor break threshold is capped between 1% and 50%. 

The major break threshold is 4/7 of the minor break threshold, while the extreme break threshold is 1/4 of that (1/7 of the minor break threshold). The base mental break threshold stat is 35%, which yields the following: 

  * Minor break threshold: 35%
  * Major break threshold: 20%
  * Extreme break threshold: 5%



### Severity[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Severity")]

There are three severity levels of mental break events, each with their own possible events and frequencies. 

  * Below their minor mental break threshold, the pawn will have a minor break MTB of 4 days.
  * Below their major break threshold, the pawn will have a major break MTB of 0.8 days.
  * Below their extreme mental break threshold, the pawn will have an extreme break MTB of 0.5 days.



### During[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: During")]

[![](/images/c/c6/Colonist_icon_while_Binging_on_Psychite.png)](/wiki/File:Colonist_icon_while_Binging_on_Psychite.png)

Colonist icon while breaking

While a colonist is experiencing a mental break, their name is displayed **in green** in the colonist bar (at the top of the game screen), and you have no direct control over them. They may enter restricted areas, consume restricted food or drugs, attack allies, or destroy objects. [Arrest](/wiki/Arrest "Arrest") the misbehaving pawn to interrupt their mischievous actions. The broken colonist has a [chance](/wiki/Arrest_Success_Chance "Arrest Success Chance") based on the arresting pawn's stats to either go berserk, or come quietly and be escorted to the nearest available prison bed - immediately ending their mental breakdown. However, arrest will downgrade their status as a member of your colony to a prisoner. Releasing them will return them to the colony, but replaces the catharsis bonus with a −6 _Was imprisoned_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 12 [days](/wiki/Time "Time"). 

Another more extreme way of ending a mental break is by attacking the offending pawn, such as by beating the pawn with [blunt-damage melee weapons](/wiki/Weapons#Melee_weapons "Weapons") until they are incapacitated by pain, or by inflicting [brain shock](/wiki/Brain_shock "Brain shock"). This is the only way to stop a berserk pawn. However, direct violence causes the victim to have a negative opinion of the colonists who beat him up, and you risk killing the pawn if he is already injured. It is ineffective if the pawn feels reduced pain, like those with the [Painstopper](/wiki/Painstopper "Painstopper") implant or high on [Go-juice](/wiki/Go-juice "Go-juice"). 

The [word of serenity](/wiki/Word_of_serenity "Word of serenity") psycast[![Content added by the Royalty DLC](/images/thumb/4/41/RoyaltyIcon.png/16px-RoyaltyIcon.png)](/wiki/Royalty_DLC "Content added by the Royalty DLC") will instantly end any mental break by forcing the target into a 6 hour coma. The psyfocus cost depends on the level of the mental break the target is experiencing. This cannot be used on [babies](/wiki/Babies "Babies")[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC") or [children](/wiki/Children "Children")[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC"). 

While having a break, at the top of the game screen, a colonist will be designated with a small "lightning bolt" icon over their image; yellow for non-aggressive breaks (such as food binge or daze), and red for aggressive ones (such as tantrum or berserk). 

### Aftermath[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Aftermath")]

Once a pawn's mental break ends, they'll revert back to their usual schedule and work and may be drafted again. Their thoughts do not 'reset,' so negative thoughts may still be present. Pawns get the 40 _Catharsis_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 3 [days](/wiki/Time "Time"). This will generally prevent a pawns from entering a spiral of mental breakdowns in all but the worst scenarios, but multiple catharses do stack up to 5 times, with diminishing effects. 

Colonists with the [Tortured artist](/wiki/Tortured_artist "Tortured artist") trait will have a constant −8 _Tortured artist_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") debuff but also have a chance (50%) to receive a creative inspiration after a mental break ends. Note that this does not include the Fleeing fire, Run wild and Catatonic breakdown breaks. 

Colonists may be considered **guilty** after particularly destructive breaks and may then be banished or executed with no, or reduced, consequences. 

### Anomalous breaks[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Anomalous breaks")]

[![AnomalyIcon.png](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/40px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Anomaly \(DLC\)") | **This section relates to content added by[Anomaly (DLC)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Anomaly \(DLC\)").** Please note that it will not be present without the [DLC](/wiki/Downloadable_content "Downloadable content") enabled.  
---|---  
  
Whenever a mental break happens, there's a chance that an anomalous break will happen over a normal one. If the game attempts to trigger anomalous mental break but is unable to, it will instead attempt to trigger a normal one. [Creepjoiners](/wiki/Creepjoiner "Creepjoiner") will always trigger an anomalous break, unless none are possible. For every other pawn, the chance for anomalous break to happen depends on the current [Anomaly stage](/wiki/Void_monolith#Summary "Void monolith"). 

Anomaly stage  | Chance   
---|---  
Inactive  | 5%   
Stirring  | 20%   
Waking  | 40%   
VoidAwakened  | 60%   
Gleaming  | 60%   
Embraced  | 20%   
Disrupted  | 0%   
  
Anomalous breaks trigger the 30 _Void catharsis_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 3 [days](/wiki/Time "Time") in place of the regular 40 _Catharsis_ [thought](/wiki/Thought "Thought") for 3 [days](/wiki/Time "Time") that other mental breaks have. The mental breaks which are considered anomalous are: 

  * Dark visions
  * Insane ramblings
  * Entity slayer
  * Entity liberator
  * Terrifying hallucinations



## Minor break[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Minor break")]

### Food binge[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Food binge")]

[![](/images/thumb/1/10/FoodBinge.png/300px-FoodBinge.png)](/wiki/File:FoodBinge.png)

[](/wiki/File:FoodBinge.png "Enlarge")

Food Binge

Causes the pawn to pig out on whatever food they can get their hands on.[Details]

This break can only occur when the map the pawn is currently on contains more than 10 human-edible nutrition in a [stockpile zone](/wiki/Stockpile_zone "Stockpile zone"), however an already ongoing binge will not end if all the food is consumed or otherwise removed. Pawns with the [Gourmand trait](/wiki/Trait#Gourmand "Trait") will experience this on average once every 50 days. 

Pawns may ignore [food policy](/wiki/Food_policy "Food policy") restrictions during this binge, and will also disregard whether a food item has been disallowed. Pawns tend to prioritize eating higher-quality food regardless of such restrictions.[Details] If there is no food, or only low quality (raw (?)) food available in storage, pawns may travel large distances across the map to eat other food on the map, including [insect jelly](/wiki/Insect_jelly "Insect jelly") that is guarded by hostile [insectoids](/wiki/Insectoids "Insectoids"). This can result in pawns being killed by insect hives. 

A binge will end as soon as the affected pawn goes to bed, or after a certain amount of time has passed. Note that if [wake-up](/wiki/Wake-up "Wake-up") is available, the pawn will take it during their break down, extending it to its maximum duration. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 25,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (10 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 45,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (18 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.8

Sad Wander

Causes the pawn to wander around aimlessly, eventually recovering on their own. Like the [Psychotic wandering (Daze)](/wiki/Mental_break#Psychotic_wandering_.28Daze.29 "Mental break") event, but shorter. 

Wandering colonists can be [arrested](/wiki/Prisoners "Prisoners"), which can be useful if they are in imminent danger. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 40,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (16 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 60,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (24 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.5

### Hide in room[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Hide in room")]

[![](/images/thumb/0/08/HideInRoom.png/300px-HideInRoom.png)](/wiki/File:HideInRoom.png)

[](/wiki/File:HideInRoom.png "Enlarge")

Hide in Room

Causes the pawn to wander about in their room, refusing to come out or satisfy their needs. 

This break can only occur if the breaking pawn has a bedroom assigned to them. 

This break is relatively short, and usually does not put the pawn in harm's way. At worst, the pawn may suffer from mild starvation or exhaustion. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 40,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (16 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 60,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (24 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.5

### Insulting spree[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Insulting spree")]

Causes the pawn [insult](/wiki/Social#Insulted "Social") nearby colonists at random, making moods worse for everyone affected. 

Insults can lead to a [social fight](/wiki/Social_fight "Social fight"), which would then lead to the end of the break. 

Requires at least two other pathable colonists to occur. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 25,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (10 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 45,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (18 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.5

### Targeted insulting spree[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Targeted insulting spree")]

Causes the pawn to follow and repeatedly [insult](/wiki/Social#Insulted "Social") one other colonist in particular. 

This can totally ruin the victim's mood with an up to −33 mood penalty through 10 stacked insults, which can potentially trigger a mental break in the victim as well. 

Insults can lead to a [social fight](/wiki/Social_fight "Social fight"), which would then lead to the end of the break. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 25,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (10 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 45,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (18 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.5

[![](/images/thumb/5/51/SocialBinge.png/300px-SocialBinge.png)](/wiki/File:SocialBinge.png)

[](/wiki/File:SocialBinge.png "Enlarge")

Social Drug Binge

Causes the pawn to drink or smoke their problems away by binging on [social drugs](/wiki/Social_drugs "Social drugs") (i.e. [ambrosia](/wiki/Ambrosia "Ambrosia"), [beer](/wiki/Beer "Beer"), [smokeleaf joints](/wiki/Smokeleaf_joint "Smokeleaf joint"), [psychite tea](/wiki/Psychite_tea "Psychite tea")).

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 40,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (16 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 60,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (24 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 1

### Psychotic wandering (Daze)[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Psychotic wandering \(Daze\)")]

[![](/images/thumb/7/79/Daze.png/300px-Daze.png)](/wiki/File:Daze.png)

[](/wiki/File:Daze.png "Enlarge")

Daze

Causes the pawn to wander around aimlessly, eventually recovering on their own. The letter informing you of this break refers to it as a "Daze." 

Affected colonists can ignore [food restrictions](/wiki/Food_restriction "Food restriction"). This can push the colony to collapse, especially with few colonists and without enough automation. 

Dazed colonists can be [arrested](/wiki/Prisoners "Prisoners"), which can be useful if they are in imminent danger. 

Unlike sad wandering, [prisoners](/wiki/Prisoners "Prisoners") can stage prison breaks whilst in this mental state. They will still wander around aimlessly, but will be able to open doors. They may still attack colonists if caught in melee. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)*   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 70,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (28 in-game hours)*   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 90,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (36 in-game hours)*   

    **Commonality:** 1 (See next)

**Note:** It has been observed that if a "dazed" colonist falls asleep, the recovery timer appears to pause until they wake up again.[Verification]

### Tantrum[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Tantrum")]

Causes the pawn to take out their anger on the structures in the colony, randomly punching at (and possibly destroying) them. The attacks may create [building rubble](/wiki/Filth#Building_rubble "Filth") in the process. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 8,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (3.2 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 12,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4.8 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.333

### Targeted tantrum[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Targeted tantrum")]

Causes the pawn to take out their anger on a single structure or stack of items, punching at them until eventually destroyed. The tantrum will redirect to another structure or stack of items if that structure or stack of items is destroyed or made inaccessible. 

Targeted tantrums are significantly more dangerous than the non-targeted variety, as the pawn has a much higher chance of actually destroying things instead of simply damaging them. They are especially dangerous when the pawn targets mortar shells (beware the [antigrain warhead](/wiki/Antigrain_warhead "Antigrain warhead")), which explode upon destruction, or if they attack important stacks such as stacks of components or medicine that the colony relies upon. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 40,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (16 in-game hours)   

    **Maximum time until Recovery:** 60,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (24 in-game hours)   

    **Commonality:** 0.333

### Bedroom tantrum[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Bedroom tantrum")]

Causes the pawn to hide in their bedroom and randomly punch at structures within.

[](/wiki/File:BerserkScreenshot.png "Enlarge")

Berserk

The pawn will lose total control, melee attacking any living creature near them. They will not intentionally kill anything, although unlucky hits may still result in death, instead redirecting to another target once their current target is downed. 

Pawns that are [incapable of violent](/wiki/Work#Incapable_of_work_types "Work") can still go berserk. Berserk colonists are able to use doors that are forbidden, and don't close doors behind them (auto-doors included). Berserk prisoners will bash at doors until they break. 

Subduing the berserk pawn with bare fists or a blunt weapon poses the least risk of killing them. Once a berserk pawn is subdued, the break is over. They do not need to be arrested, and can be immediately rescued to recover from their injuries. If the berserker is not in danger and can be safely avoided, it can be best to leave them to rampage by themselves until their berserk rage wears off to avoid injuries. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)

Fire-starting sprees are very short and can easily be dealt with by having somebody else "babysit" the pyromaniac, putting out fires as the pyromaniac pawn starts them. However, it is worthwhile to consider that they may also target volatile objects such as [chemfuel](/wiki/Chemfuel "Chemfuel").

Since catatonic breakdown is technically a health condition, usage of the [healer mech serum](/wiki/Healer_mech_serum "Healer mech serum") or [unnatural healing](/wiki/Unnatural_healing "Unnatural healing")[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC") can instantly bring the affected colonist out of the mental break.

This mental break does not trigger the [Inspired creativity](/wiki/Inspired_creativity "Inspired creativity") inspiration from the [Tortured artist](/wiki/Tortured_artist "Tortured artist") trait.

### Run wild[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Run wild")]

Causes the pawn to run into the wild and start acting like an animal. While acting like an animal, the pawn will subsist on wild sources of food, usually berry bushes or agave in arid biomes. They may wander across spike traps and randomly trigger them. 

Unlike other breaks, this one lasts indefinitely without intervention. The only way to end it is to have another colonist tame the broken colonist and convince them to act like a human again. Taming a wild man is equivalent to taming an animal of 75% wildness, equivalent to an [elephant](/wiki/Elephant "Elephant") or [fox](/wiki/Fox "Fox"), and requiring an [Animals](/wiki/Skills#Animals "Skills") skill of 7 or greater. 

Damaging them until they are downed isn't an advisable option; since they don't belong to your faction anymore, they are at risk of dying upon receiving enough damage to become incapacitated. Using a [psychic shock lance](/wiki/Psychic_shock_lance "Psychic shock lance") to down them will ignore the [death on downed](/wiki/Death_on_downed "Death on downed") chance, however there is always the risk of brain damage. If rescued, they have a chance to join you upon recovery. 

Upon taming, they will have their usual Catharsis moodlet, and will have their Beauty and Comfort needs set to 50%, while their Outdoors needs is set to 100%. 

It is ten times less likely to occur in a colony with a population of two or less, and it increases linearly to the normal level at a population of seven or more. It cannot occur during [toxic fallout](/wiki/Toxic_fallout "Toxic fallout") or [acidic smog](/wiki/Acidic_smog "Acidic smog"). 

Additionally, this mental break does not trigger the [Inspired creativity](/wiki/Inspired_creativity "Inspired creativity") inspiration from the [Tortured artist](/wiki/Tortured_artist "Tortured artist") trait. 

    **Commonality:** 0.5

Social fighting is triggered when one pawn slights or insults another (significantly more likely with insulting than slighting), and the affected pawn gets angered. ### Fleeing in panic[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=45 "Edit section: Fleeing in panic")]

[![](/images/thumb/3/3d/Fleeing_in_panic.png/300px-Fleeing_in_panic.png)](/wiki/File:Fleeing_in_panic.png)

[](/wiki/File:Fleeing_in_panic.png "Enlarge")

Fleeing in panic

This sort of mental break happens when half (rounded up) of a group of non-colonist humans are killed; whether it be hostiles, traders, or the like. Pawns affected by this mental state will attempt to leave the map by any means possible as soon as they realize that they're not going to win whatever battle they got caught up in. 

This mental state will end once the affected pawn leaves the map, or when they're killed. 

Pawns in this state will run indiscriminately towards the closest edge of the map, completely disregarding any hostiles in the way (such as your colonists firing at them), trying to smash down doors even when there is a clear path out next to them.

### Manhunter[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=47 "Edit section: Manhunter")]

For the [event](/wiki/Event "Event") that causes a pack of animals affected by this break to spawn on the map, see [Manhunter pack](/wiki/Manhunter_pack "Manhunter pack").

Animals may suffer a manhunter mental break, similar to [berserk](/wiki/Mental_break#Berserk "Mental break"). A manhunter animal will seek out and attack any humans and [Mechanoids](/wiki/Mechanoids "Mechanoids"). Animal pulser maddened animals will attack mechs on the map it can reach, regardless of faction, but will ignore tamed animals. 

Manhunters will not spontaneously attempt to break through obstacles to hunt down pawns, but will attack doors if they see someone go through them. They will cease their attack after a short period, but may still successfully break it down in that time. If strong doors are not available to hide behind, try to get out of line of sight before ducking into a nearby building, be ready to repair the door from the inside, or simply accept that the door is only a temporary barrier. 

The mental break can be triggered in several ways: 

  * Via the [Mad animal](/wiki/Mad_animal "Mad animal") event. Affects 1 wild animal.
  * Via the [Psychic wave](/wiki/Psychic_wave "Psychic wave") event. Affects all wild animals on the relevant map.
  * Via the use of a [psychic animal pulser](/wiki/Psychic_animal_pulser "Psychic animal pulser"). Affects all non-player owned animals on the relevant map.
  * Via the use of [Manhunter Pulse](/wiki/Manhunter_pulse "Manhunter pulse"). Affects all animals (including player-owned animals) within the target radius.
  * Via injury inflicted by any human pawn. Chance is controlled by the animal's "Manhunter Chance", the [hunting stealth](/wiki/Hunting_stealth "Hunting stealth") of the injuring pawn, and the distance between the pawn and the animal. Affects the injured animal, and in the case of certain herd animals, any nearby animals of the same species.
  * Via a failed taming attempt angering the animal. Chance is controlled by the animal's "Manhunter Chance (Taming)". Affects the targeted wild animal.[Herd?]
  * Via the death of their [bonded](/wiki/Bond "Bond") master.[Chance?] Affects any tamed and bonded animal



An animal's manhunting rampage will end after a certain amount of time, upon the animal sleeping, or once [downed](/wiki/Downed "Downed"). 

Manhunter animals with [Scaria](/wiki/Scaria "Scaria") can not be [tamed](/wiki/Tame "Tame") without first curing them. Attempts to [prioritize](/wiki/Prioritize "Prioritize") the taming task will result instead in a hint saying _Animal busy_. 

    **Mean time between Recovery:** 18,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (7.2 in-game hours)   

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 10,000 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (4 in-game hours)   


### Manhunter Permanent[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=48 "Edit section: Manhunter Permanent")]

Permanent manhunter is randomly applied to animals that are part of a [manhunter pack](/wiki/Manhunter_pack "Manhunter pack"). Affected animals will never snap out of this mental state under normal circumstances, and completely neglect their own needs. Animals in this state will generally leave the area within a few days. 

Some manhunter animals, such as [boomrats](/wiki/Boomrat "Boomrat"), will typically starve to death due to their hunger rate combined with the typical manhunter animal duration. 

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 99,999,999 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (27.78 years)   


### Roaming[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=49 "Edit section: Roaming")]

Introduced in 1.3, farm animals (animal species that cannot be controlled via zones) can randomly get this mental break if they are outside of an enclosed pen. This can cause them to leave the map if not roped in immediately. If they are enclosed in a pen, or if they are roped in by a colonist after a valid pen is placed, this mental break will immediately end. 

This does not occur on maps where they have recently been brought in, or on maps the other colonist are raiding in. 

    **Minimum time until Recovery:** 99,999,999 [ticks](/wiki/Time "Time") (27.78 years)

### Cube sculpting[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=52 "Edit section: Cube sculpting")]

[![AnomalyIcon.png](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/40px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Anomaly \(DLC\)") | **This section relates to content added by[Anomaly (DLC)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Anomaly \(DLC\)").** Please note that it will not be present without the [DLC](/wiki/Downloadable_content "Downloadable content") enabled.

Randomly happens in pawns with an interest in the [Golden cube](/wiki/Golden_cube "Golden cube"), where they will build a sculpture on the ground in the shape of a cube. This may be made of sand, dirt, or miscellaneous scrap, depending on the surface. The break will then immediately end. 

It is important to mention that these sculptures are affected by [quality](/wiki/Quality "Quality"), therefore a pawn with an [Inspired creativity](/wiki/Inspired_creativity "Inspired creativity") inspiration may use their inspiration on a cube sculpture.

## Tips[[edit](/index.php?title=Mental_break&action=edit&section=53 "Edit section: Tips")]

  * [Arrested](/wiki/Arrest "Arrest") [prisoners](/wiki/Prisoner "Prisoner") and [prisons](/wiki/Prison "Prison") are more expensive than random damage of most mental breaks, imprisoning is usually more worth it if the mental break needs to end quickly such as during raids and when something needs to be dealt with immediately.
  * Pawns during a mental break are immune to damage from [Unnatural darkness](/wiki/Unnatural_darkness "Unnatural darkness"),[![Content added by the Anomaly DLC](/images/thumb/3/32/AnomalyIcon.png/16px-AnomalyIcon.png)](/wiki/Anomaly_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Anomaly DLC") however once the mental break ends this immunity subsequently ends as well.
  * [Wimps](/wiki/Wimp "Wimp") are easier to deal with during their mental breaks if avoiding imprisonment, because the lower pain shock threshold makes them be downed with less injury. The [extra pain](/wiki/Extra_pain "Extra pain") gene[![Content added by the Biotech DLC](/images/thumb/7/73/BiotechIcon.png/16px-BiotechIcon.png)](/wiki/Biotech_\(DLC\) "Content added by the Biotech DLC") can be installed on pawns to add the trait.
  * Pawns with [artificial body parts](/wiki/Artificial_body_parts "Artificial body parts") that are subject to [brain shock](/wiki/Brain_shock "Brain shock") can be easily, safely, and instantly downed upon any exposure to [EMP](/wiki/EMP "EMP"). Note that as of the time of writing, the only parts that apply this effect are from the [Royalty](/wiki/Royalty "Royalty") DLC.
