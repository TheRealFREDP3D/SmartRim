[![Content exclusively from the PC Edition](/images/thumb/8/89/PC.png/16px-PC.png)](/wiki/PC_edition "Content exclusively from the PC Edition") _Released on February 24, 2020_

**Royalty** is a paid [DLC](/wiki/DLC "DLC") for RimWorld that introduces [royal titles](/wiki/Titles "Titles"), [psycasting](/wiki/Psycast "Psycast"), a new [faction](/wiki/Empire "Empire"), new gear, and a new threat in the form of [mech clusters](/wiki/Mech_cluster "Mech cluster"). It was released on February 24, 2020, alongside Version 1.1.

## Description[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Description")]

The [Empire](/wiki/Empire "Empire") has arrived. Their refugee fleet settles the rimworld, and seeks allies. Their honor-bound culture wields hyper-advanced technology, while bowing to the ancient traditions of kings and queens. 

## The Empire & Nobility[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: The Empire & Nobility")]

Main article: [Empire](/wiki/Empire "Empire")

The [empire](/wiki/Empire "Empire") is a new ultratech faction - the main headline of the Royalty DLC. They make for powerful allies, or if you choose to [betray them](/wiki/Quests#The_Deserter "Quests"), powerful enemies. All empire soldiers are implanted with a [death acidifier](/wiki/Death_acidifier "Death acidifier"), preventing players from (easily) looting their high-tech gear. 

### Titles[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Titles")]

Main article: [Titles](/wiki/Titles "Titles")

[Titles](/wiki/Title "Title") are bestowed by the empire as a sign of nobility. Having a higher title gives you many privileges with the empire, but comes with increasing demands. 

In order to rise up the noble ranks, your colonists must gain [honor](/wiki/Honor "Honor"), a new currency entirely separate from [goodwill](/wiki/Goodwill "Goodwill"). The primary way to get honor is to do [quests](/wiki/Quest "Quest") for the empire. You can also get honor by selling [gold](/wiki/Gold "Gold") or [prisoners](/wiki/Prisoner "Prisoner") to a [royal tribute collector](/wiki/Royal_tribute_collector "Royal tribute collector"). 

Noble colonists become haughty and demanding. In order to receive a noble rank at all, you must have a [throne room](/wiki/Titles#Thrones_and_Bedrooms "Titles") that meets the royal requirements. While most noble colonists remain willing to do all types of jobs, they'll demand royal clothes and luxurious bedrooms. In constrast, the empire's nobles are _[conceited](/wiki/Titles#Conceited_pawns "Titles")_. They'll refuse to do some types of work or eat commoner food. Colonists with the [greedy](/wiki/Greedy "Greedy"), [jealous](/wiki/Jealous "Jealous"), or [abrasive](/wiki/Abrasive "Abrasive") traits will also become conceited, and come with the same demands. 

In exchange, a title comes with various perks. The first is a [psylink](/wiki/Psylink "Psylink") given by the empire, allowing access to special [psycast](/wiki/Psycast "Psycast") powers. With [permits](/wiki/Permit "Permit"), you can call in the empire for direct help - this can range aerial bombardment, to an [imperial shuttle](/wiki/Imperial_shuttle "Imperial shuttle"), to a direct drop of [glitterworld medicine](/wiki/Glitterworld_medicine "Glitterworld medicine"). Only pawns of a sufficient title (Knight / Dame) will be able to trade with the empire's [faction bases](/wiki/Faction_base "Faction base"), and only pawns of at least Baron rank can interact with the empire's [trade ships](/wiki/Trade_ship "Trade ship"). 

## Psycasting[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Psycasting")]

Main article: [Psycasts](/wiki/Psycasts "Psycasts")

[Psycasts](/wiki/Psycasts "Psycasts") are _psionic_ abilities, manipulating reality beyond human understanding. They are "psychic" like how [psychic drones](/wiki/Psychic_drone "Psychic drone") and [psychic soothes](/wiki/Psychic_soothe "Psychic soothe") are psychic. 

The vast majority of psycasts are _not_ direct, damage-dealing abilities. Instead, they focus on new ways to inhibit enemies or change the flow of combat. [Blinding pulse](/wiki/Blinding_pulse "Blinding pulse") lowers enemies' sight. [Skip](/wiki/Skip "Skip") allows you to move a _thing_ \- friend, enemy, or item - anywhere within range. Other psycasts are useful outside of combat. [Word of love](/wiki/Word_of_love "Word of love") creates romantic attraction, while [word of serenity](/wiki/Word_of_serenity "Word of serenity") ends social fights and [mental breaks](/wiki/Mental_break "Mental break"). No enemy in the game will ever use any form of psycast, even if they have the requisite psylink. 

In combat, psycasts are limited by [neural heat](/wiki/Neural_heat "Neural heat"). You can overload on neural heat, at the cost of [gimping](/wiki/Psychic_breakdown "Psychic breakdown") your caster for a few days afterward. 

Outside of combat, psycasts are limited by [psyfocus](/wiki/Psyfocus "Psyfocus"). In order to replenish psyfocus, your casters must [meditate](/wiki/Meditation "Meditation"). Higher-level psycasters will have to meditate for hours every day. Nobles can meditate on their [throne](/wiki/Throne "Throne"). [Pyromaniacs](/wiki/Pyromaniac "Pyromaniac") can meditate to fire. [Psychopaths](/wiki/Psychopath "Psychopath") can meditate to [graves](/wiki/Grave "Grave") of their loved ones. Meditating counts as [recreation](/wiki/Recreation "Recreation"), though like all recreation, colonists will get bored of constant meditation. 

The empire is one way to get [psylinks](/wiki/Psylink "Psylink"), though they can be obtained through a few other ways. Most [tribal](/wiki/Tribal "Tribal") pawns - those with _Natural_ meditation - have a special connection with the [anima tree](/wiki/Anima_tree "Anima tree"). If they meditate at the tree for enough time, they can undergo a [linking ritual](/wiki/Anima_tree#Linking_ritual "Anima tree"). 

You can also get [psylink neuroformers](/wiki/Psylink_neuroformer "Psylink neuroformer") from quests. [The Deserter](/wiki/Quests#The_Deserter "Quests") quest will allow you to betray the empire for an opportunity to gain 2 psylink neuroformers. Note that there is no special "betray the empire" questline or mechanic, beyond the aforementioned quest. The empire can be re-allied with enough gifts or peace talks. 

## Mechanoid clusters[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Mechanoid clusters")]

Main article: [Mech cluster](/wiki/Mech_cluster "Mech cluster"), and [Condition causer](/wiki/Condition_causer "Condition causer")

Mechanoids have evolved. [Mech clusters](/wiki/Mech_cluster "Mech cluster") are a new type of [major threat](/wiki/Major_threat "Major threat") \- stationary encampments which present a new tactical challenge. Like [crashed ship parts](/wiki/Crashed_ship_parts "Crashed ship parts"), mech clusters always start dormant, but you may have to deal with them immediately. They put the player on the offensive, which contrasts with the defense-oriented fights in the base game. 

You can split mech clusters into 2 parts. 

  * The first part is the [condition causer](/wiki/Condition_causer "Condition causer"). These can range from [toxic spewers](/wiki/Toxic_spewer "Toxic spewer"), which create an everlasting [toxic fallout](/wiki/Toxic_fallout "Toxic fallout"), to [EMI dynamos](/wiki/EMI_dynamo "EMI dynamo"), which disable all electric devices. These serve as incentive for you to destroy the cluster. But you don't _have_ to. In any case, not all mech clusters come with a condition causer.
  * The second part is the structure of the cluster itself. Mech clusters can come with [walls](/wiki/Wall "Wall"), explosive [unstable power cells](/wiki/Unstable_power_cell "Unstable power cell"), and [turrets](/wiki/Turret "Turret"). They can be protected by [mech low-shields](/wiki/Mech_low-shield "Mech low-shield") or [mech high-shields](/wiki/Mech_high-shield "Mech high-shield"), preventing gunfire or mortar fire until disabled (respectively).



And, of course, clusters come with [mechanoids](/wiki/Mechanoid "Mechanoid"). They can come sleeping, hidden in a [mech capsule](/wiki/Mech_capsule "Mech capsule"), or produced by a [mech assembler](/wiki/Mech_assembler "Mech assembler"). 

Mech clusters start dormant. Unless you shoot at a mech or build near the cluster, they won't wake up on their own. [Count-down activators](/wiki/Count-down_activator "Count-down activator") can wake mechs after a certain period of time. [Proximity activators](/wiki/Proximity_activator "Proximity activator") will wake mechs if you enter its radius. In any case, the condition causer will activate even if the rest of the mech cluster remains dormant. 

Due to their unique structures, mech clusters offer a wide variety of tactical engagement. Sometimes, [mortars](/wiki/Mortar "Mortar") are the best option. If a [mech high-shield](/wiki/Mech_high-shield "Mech high-shield") is in place, a different sort of strategy is in order. A few can be defeated with a key sniper shot to a critical structure. And who says _you_ need to deal with the cluster? With the right set up, [raiders](/wiki/Raider "Raider") can be lured to fight the cluster for you. 

In addition, both mechs and pirates can deploy condition causers from far away. You must [caravan](/wiki/Caravan "Caravan") or [transport pod](/wiki/Transport_pod "Transport pod") to its location, destroy the condition causers, and get out. 

## Quests[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Quests")]

Main article: [Quests](/wiki/Quests "Quests")

The Royalty DLC adds a lot of quests. This DLC adds more quests than what the base game had. 

Some are simple. Accept a [raid](/wiki/Raid "Raid") for a reward. Build a structure, for a reward. Others are more intrinsically complex. Host a noble, derpy animals, or desperate refugees. A quest may ask you to fight a huge mechanoid cluster, but also include help from elite Imperial cataphracts to make the battle winnable. 

## Items[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Items")]

The Royalty DLC comes with a variety of high-tech gear, implants, and other items. 

  * New ultratech melee weapons. Royalty introduces the [monosword](/wiki/Monosword "Monosword"), [zeushammer](/wiki/Zeushammer "Zeushammer"), and [plasmasword](/wiki/Plasmasword "Plasmasword"), and their [persona](/wiki/Persona_weapon "Persona weapon") variants. Persona weapons come with unique traits that can hinder or help their wielder.
  * New armor. [Cataphract armor](/wiki/Cataphract_armor "Cataphract armor") is the new heaviest [power armor](/wiki/Power_armor "Power armor") in the game. Royalty introduces 3 variants of existing power armor - the [locust armor](/wiki/Locust_armor "Locust armor"), [grenadier armor](/wiki/Grenadier_armor "Grenadier armor"), and [phoenix armor](/wiki/Phoenix_armor "Phoenix armor"), each providing a unique ability for a small stat penalty. [Prestige armor](/wiki/Prestige_armor "Prestige armor") is another variant of power armor - mostly identical to their base variant, but pleases [nobles](/wiki/Noble "Noble").
  * A large amount of [artificial body parts](/wiki/Artificial_body_part "Artificial body part"). [Drill arms](/wiki/Drill_arm "Drill arm") and [field hands](/wiki/Field_hand "Field hand") improve their associated work speed drastically. [Neurocalculators](/wiki/Neurocalculator "Neurocalculator") and [learning assistants](/wiki/Learning_assistant "Learning assistant") help with mental tasks. [Circadian half-cyclers](/wiki/Circadian_half-cycler "Circadian half-cycler") will make your pawns never sleep again - though at a steep [Consciousness](/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness") penalty.
  * [Utility](/wiki/Utility "Utility") items. [Low-shield packs](/wiki/Low-shield_pack "Low-shield pack") protect against _all_ bullets for its duration, unless it is hit by [EMP](/wiki/EMP "EMP"). [Jump packs](/wiki/Jump_pack "Jump pack") allow a user to fly across a battlefield.



Many items introduced in Royalty, like cataphract armor, requires a [techprint](/wiki/Techprint "Techprint") before you can research and create them yourself. The ultratech melee weapons cannot be created by your colony at all, only traded. 

## Royal Ascent[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Royal Ascent")]

Main article: [Royal Ascent](/wiki/Royal_Ascent "Royal Ascent")

The [Royal Ascent](/wiki/Royal_Ascent "Royal Ascent") quest is a new endgame quest provided by the Royalty DLC. The High Stellarch - the de-facto leader of the empire - can offer your colony a way off the planet, if their royal demands are met. 

Once you have a pawn with the Archon [title](/wiki/Title "Title"), you can request a customary visit from the High Stellarch, along with 4 imperial guards. This visit lasts for 12 days. Like the [ship launch](/wiki/Ship_launch "Ship launch"), you'll have to contend with constant [raids](/wiki/Raid "Raid"), from those who want to dethrone the stellarch. An additional challenge is the stellarch themselves - you must keep them happy enough throughout the stay. 

## Music[[edit](/index.php?title=Royalty_\(DLC\)&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Music")]

Main article: [Soundtrack](/wiki/Soundtrack "Soundtrack")

This expansion includes a new full album worth of new music by Alistair Lindsay, composer of the original RimWorld soundtrack. 

There are 13 new tracks totaling 1:04:14 in length.
